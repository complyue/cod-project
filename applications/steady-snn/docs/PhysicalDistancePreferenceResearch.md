## Assessment

**Our neurons are already organized by their assigned semantic-vector roles. This supplies no natural physical distance between them.** Neither embedding dimension numbers, within-minicolumn cell numbers nor storage addresses define an anatomical metric `r_ij`.

The biological studies below describe systems that possess physical anatomy. They support distinctions among anatomical contact, functional selection and recurrent graph structure in those systems. They do **not** establish that our model should add a soft distance prior, explicit positions or an effective contact graph.

The research question is therefore: **what useful computational effect does biological locality provide, and can that effect be justified within our existing semantic organization?** A benefit may depend on wiring cost, delays, graph structure, learning or their interaction. Each needs evidence; none is supplied simply by calling some neurons neighbors. No current result selects a distance rule for the application.

H-59 / AD-053 further requires normal recurrent states to remain steerable by external token input. Persistence or a repeatedly visited ensemble alone is not a stale condition. A mechanism's usefulness must include appropriate input-driven redirection; failure of every external token input to redirect the recurrence is the stale condition. [K-76](../../../design/DesignAuthorityRegistry.md#k-76), C-20/P-42 and candidate C-21/P-43/P-44 record this requirement and a possible reset recovery operation. No reset policy follows from the literature review.

### What the objectives constrain

| Confirmed objective | Consequence for connection selection | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |

The response duration needs specified dynamics. |
| A positive semantic residual grows under noise support, peaks, then fades | Learning must preserve meaningful residual amplification together with eventual exit. | A compact patch of positive-valued cells. Semantic valence and coordinate polarity differ. |

| DMN has broad qualified temporal coverage and slow negative-semantic drift | Participation must spread over time while preserving the intended semantic bias. | Simultaneous activation of most cells, or a spatial diffusion rule. |
| All normal states remain externally steerable | Retention must coexist with effective input responses; self-reinforcement alone is not success. | That clustering always causes stale, or that geometrically short connections prevent it. |
| Opposite polarities within a semantic dimension cancel intrinsically | This particular interaction has an explicit semantic relation. | Physical adjacency, arbitrary cross-dimension distances or a nearest-neighbor rule for learned edges. |

**Low neuron coverage can coexist with full semantic-dimension coverage.** As a counting example, 12 qualified participants out of 120 in each of 5,120 dimensions give 61,440 participants, or 10% of the CC, while touching every dimension. This is not an adopted encoding, instantaneous activity level or detector threshold. It shows why the small footprints of flow and immersion do not imply a small geometric neighborhood. Their distinction still depends on temporal organization; coverage alone does not separate them.

The goals also do not identify a unique placement: consistently relabeling neuron identities, edges, state, encoder assignments and intrinsic cancellation relations preserves the specified semantic responses and coverage/path observables. This is an identifiability argument, not permission to change the semantic basis and not proof that a geometric training bias cannot improve learning. Fixed identities can acquire different recurrent roles without acquiring anatomical coordinates.

### What the evidence supports

- **Functional selection beyond contact:** [Ding et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11981947/) find feature-related synaptic selectivity beyond axon–dendrite co-travel in mouse visual cortex (LOC-029/AUD-079). This motivates studying useful activity relationships separately from physical opportunity. It does not turn a coordinate index, a token-table correlation or a cell's assigned scalar input into a proven anatomical distance.
- **Temporal behavior without a spatial metric:** [Gillett and Brunel, 2024](https://elifesciences.org/articles/88805) obtain persistent/sequential behavior and input-controlled retrieval speed using heterogeneous temporal learning rules on random structural connectivity (LOC-081). The prescribed patterns and rate-model assumptions do not establish our early SNN. The narrower implication is that timing and transition preferences need not be implemented as spatial proximity.
- **Geometry remains a legitimate comparison:** [Mackey et al., 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44387-025-00013-x) report benefits from geometric regularization and pruning, with task-dependent results and no clear preferred geometry in their language comparison (LOC-257/AUD-101). Learned hidden representations, dense training and pruning differ from our fixed roles and static sparse resources. The study supports testing a concrete benefit, not selecting 1D or 2D here.

The completed source reviews also contain positive fixed-tuning and persistent-response models, including Eckmann and Kennedy. Their explicit inhibitory circuitry and other dynamical assumptions must remain visible: early-model H-54 excludes explicit inhibitory synaptic connections. Biological locality can constrain contact opportunities and wiring cost, while recurrent topology can affect computation; neither contribution automatically transfers to this CC's representation.

### Research worth continuing

The [training strategy](TrainingStrategy.md#unified-next-step-repair) owns the unified DMN/token construction and repair proposal. Expected activation, naturally achieved participation and missing candidates define the maintenance question; later Hebbian flow has its own update semantics. Immersion is assessed as emergence, without a dedicated training objective.

The next useful questions concern **which partners are encountered, which interactions are reinforced, and what behavior that reinforcement produces**:

1. **Meaningful coactivity under background noise.** Determine what evidence should favor a connection when incidental noise, noise-assisted residual growth and task-evoked recurrence coexist. H-58's filtering requirement concerns detection; it does not already select a plasticity filter. Rejecting every noise-assisted activation would conflict with the desired residual response.
2. **Recurrent support, transition direction and exits.** Determine how learning rewards persistence when appropriate while retaining token-driven redirection and finite fading. `A → B → C → A` and `A → C → B → A` can have identical instantaneous pattern frequencies and coactivity while requiring different transitions (ANA-027). Temporal evidence, potentially already present in cell dynamics, matters; this observation selects no trace array or update formula.
3. **Bounded partner discovery.** The static pool bounds reserved storage, not the cost of searching 614,400 possible cells. Investigate how useful candidates can be encountered within a finite work budget while respecting dimension neutrality. Functional preferences need not be symmetric, transitive or representable by a distance. A learned similarity graph, sampling rule, module layout or all-pairs search is not presumed.

Broad further searches for a universal anatomical map or an ideal spatial dimension have lower expected value until one of these questions identifies a specific benefit. A geometric proposal becomes worth deeper comparison if it offers a measurable candidate-search saving, useful dynamics beyond matched nonspatial controls, or a demonstrated execution/communication saving. Memory-address locality and biological axon length require separate cost evidence.

Any later comparison should preserve the semantic encoder, intrinsic cancellation, noise feedback and detection conditions; match allocated capacity, actual degree/exposure, effective weight budgets, precision and learning work as appropriate; and measure token response, residual growth/peak/fade, regime-specific coverage/path and external redirection. A benefit caused by changed input mapping, degree, weights or noise control cannot be assigned to geometry alone. This is a comparison outline, not authorization to run experiments or choose auxiliary structures.

An abstract contact graph would itself be a new modeling assumption and would not create a physical metric merely by being called a contact graph.

## What the evidence does and does not transfer

For interpreting a biological study, let `O_ij` mean anatomical contact opportunity, `S_ij` an actual synapse, `r` a measured physical descriptor and `h` relevant activity history. If a synapse requires opportunity, then:

```text
P(S_ij | r, h) = P(O_ij | r, h) * P(S_ij | O_ij, r, h)
```

This is conditional probability accounting within that description, not an application transition law or a reason to add an opportunity variable. It assumes no independence: activity can influence both anatomy and selection. Our current model has no physical `r` to substitute into it.

Two descriptive profiles used in the literature are:

```text
exponential:  K(r) = exp(-r / ell)
Gaussian:     K(r) = exp(-r^2 / (2*sigma^2))
```

A per-pair connection profile is not the same as an edge-length distribution: the number of available pairs at each radius also matters. These distance scales are unrelated to the approximately 3 exponent in our capacity-class discussion. Neither formula is an application candidate with a defined argument; choosing its scale or calling `r` dimensionless would not repair the missing meaning of distance.

Research must separate effects requiring material anatomy from effects attributable to the resulting graph or learning dynamics. The nonspatial clustered-network result above is one reason this distinction matters: useful recurrence need not prove a need for physical positions. It also does not approve a clustering algorithm for this application.

Live connections, learned weights and allocated capacity remain distinct. A degree distribution in biological tissue does not establish a distribution of reserved slots, and a locality result does not select a weight-scaling rule. Training strategy retains responsibility for effective weights under the existing capacity constraints.

## Compatibility with fixed semantic roles

Dense token vectors and non-bijective feature/minicolumn correspondence are compatible with fixed coordinate roles. The semantic arrangement is the starting model, not an incomplete anatomical layout awaiting extra coordinates.

Research must preserve these boundaries:

1. **Semantic role remains assigned:** coordinate, polarity and eventual contextual role define how a cell participates in representing a vector.
2. **No physical metric is given:** semantic grouping supplies neither micrometers nor an arbitrary geometric proxy with demonstrated meaning.
3. **Learning is still open:** useful multidimensional recurrence must be assessed against the requested behaviors. Functional association, graph adjacency and anatomical proximity are different quantities.

There is no current decision between explicit physical positions and an effective contact abstraction. Either would first need a concrete application rationale. A learned similarity metric, common-neighbor rule or other graph mechanism would also be a new proposal, not an automatic substitute for the undefined distance. H-41 rules out distance from dimension labels; its complete probability-level neutrality requirement remains unresolved as recorded in H-41/K-34. The shared internal minicolumn-position mechanism remains rejected.

## The very-near exception

H-40 includes a small very-near exception before the monotonic preference applies. Its interpretation still needs to distinguish exemption from the preference rule from a ban on connections. The reviewed physiology supports decreasing connection probability over sampled distances, not a universal inter-neuron dead zone.

Soma packing, exclusion of self-connections, a neurite contact threshold and an algorithmic rule suppressing redundant partners address different questions. None automatically supplies the others. If the exception is retained to prevent unhelpful local reinforcement, it needs that computational justification and comparison; no paper here establishes its radius or shows that every cell in the same minicolumn should be excluded. This assessment does not silently remove the user's direction or select a replacement rule.

[Oh et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2014.12.016) deliberately excluded unstimulated spines within 1.5 micrometers of a stimulated spine to avoid glutamate spillover (LOC-169). That is an experimental selection rule, not evidence of a biological dead zone. The study concerns synapses on one target dendrite, which is also a different distance from separation between neurons.

Without a justified application metric, a geometric exception radius cannot currently be assigned.

## What would count as useful for the three regimes

Any proposed computational benefit of locality must be relevant to behavior under the [broad per-CC activity envelope](ActivationRegimes.md), with state-specific activity levels allowed:

- **DMN:** maintain recurrent activity with changing distributed semantic content. Geometry alone supplies no negative-semantic valence or drift direction.
- **Flow:** allow successive token patterns to recruit and transition between assemblies. A local spatial wave is not sufficient evidence of correct token-sequence behavior.
- **Immersion:** support self-sustaining semantic recurrence with the intended range of chain lengths. Functional cycle length, elapsed time and the circumference of a geometric loop are not interchangeable.

H-52 adds convergence from varied entry conditions as the primary learning objective and token-by-token self-recurrence and gradual fading as an intermediate target; H-67 permits a loose half-life-like scale and expected variation. A locality proposal should therefore improve the appropriate attraction, transient response or return behavior under comparable input and resource conditions. Stronger persistence alone need not improve a response that must fade; the token duration is not a required lifetime for immersion.

H-53 makes background drive a required comparison condition: attention substantially suppresses white noise, the isolated response fades under low noise, and noise is raised after DMN detection. H-55 additionally raises noise when cancellation reduces active fraction, before DMN detection when needed; the positive residual should grow to a peak and then fade. Any claimed geometry benefit must be distinguished from changing that drive or its control policy. Increased DMN noise supports activity; the slow convergence toward negative meaning still requires the specified semantic bias, independent of coordinate sign.

Research should identify which effects survive when physical wiring cost, spatial delays or anatomical restrictions are absent, and which depend on those properties. When a study attributes an effect to locality, inspect whether differences in degree, weights, common input, motifs or learning could explain it instead. A matched anatomical distance histogram alone is not application acceptance.

The approximate token-response duration is a human-specified behavioral target; experimental protocols, numeric pass/fail tolerances and runtime bookkeeping remain open. No positions, contact arrays, graph indexes, replacement semantic roles or additional memory budgets are introduced. A research result must explain a useful effect and its transfer conditions within the current semantic organization; it need not end by recommending a spatial model.

H-56 defines flexible-window coverage and activation-path criteria for detecting and training the regimes. Total active fraction remains a regulation variable and is excluded from classification. H-57 rules out flow above a small short-window coverage cap (20% is illustrative); H-58 requires filtering incidental white-noise participation, with a density gate proposed. A geometry comparison must preserve the same qualified-coverage, window and path definitions.

The relevant distinction is what a spatial construction actually does. A cheaper physical cable layout, a useful dynamical mode, a smoother learned representation and a better prediction of brain measurements answer different questions. These comparisons do not make physical locality irrelevant. They identify the missing link: which effect is needed for the specified semantic behavior, and which assumptions are necessary to obtain it. Geometry is neither automatically justified by biological resemblance nor ruled out by the absence of natural coordinates.

The spatial reservoir result of [Pyle and Rosenbaum, 2017](https://robertrosenbaum.github.io/robertrosenbaum/Papers/PylePRL17Combined.pdf) provides a useful positive case with an important task boundary (LOC-018). Spatially heterogeneous drive improves trained readout performance, but recalling an output after the input ends benefits from an added adaptation process; the spatial input alone performs poorly in that delayed condition. The nonspatial comparison also changes input organization, so it does not isolate recurrent geometry alone. Useful driven temporal modes are established; persistent semantic recall needs additional evidence.

The deeper [Ding et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08840-3) analysis distinguishes two controls (LOC-029/AUD-079). Individual feature-selectivity models and the joint null model for common postsynaptic input answer different questions. The latter combines multiple functional and anatomical predictors and still leaves excess common input in several projection classes. This is stronger than merely observing nearby coactivity. Its contact-length exposure, measured response similarity and single-mouse anatomy remain supplied biological quantities; the result does not turn signed embedding-coordinate labels into those quantities. The accompanying trained recurrent example uses learned input mappings and dense recurrence, so its functional ablations do not directly establish our fixed-role sparse transfer.

The fitted static Green function and its thresholded adjacency do not identify a neuronal update rule. Postprocessing can change the eigenbasis; anatomical fit is not evidence of sustained semantic loops. |
| **Topology predicts stimulated propagation beyond distance:** [Seguin et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2023.01.027), LOC-111 | Direct stimulation data support contributions from distributed structural pathways beyond spatial proximity. Distance-preserving rewiring and nonlinear-distance controls are part of the evidence. | The measurements concern macroscopic human regions. Predictive communication statistics do not uniquely identify the neuronal signaling mechanism or prescribe a microscopic learning rule. |
| **Geometry and connectivity contribute differently across cortex:** [Royer et al., published online 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-67740-2), LOC-114 | Anatomical proximity is the strongest tested global correlate of electrophysiological spectral similarity; microstructure and connectivity add more explanatory value in transmodal regions. | This is regional, correlational evidence. The plotted response space uses two dimensions, but the analysis retains fourteen gradients for 50% of its variance. That response space is not the anatomical sheet or a semantic-dimension layout. |

These results make anatomy informative without making a universal distance rule sufficient. In particular, retaining an empirical graph can retain anatomical effects even when a simulation no longer reads coordinates. That is different from demonstrating that the graph could be learned from our semantic input without a geometric prior.

[Miner and Triesch, 2014](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnana.2014.00125) show that slicing and sampling a supplied spatial graph can change apparent reciprocity, common-neighbor association and motif enrichment (LOC-230). Their model explains part of the observations while leaving directional and stronger clustering effects unexplained. This supports better anatomical controls rather than dismissing all selective wiring as a sampling artifact.

[Vegué, Perin and Roxin, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0984-17.2017) directly reanalyse the small-sample inference problem (LOC-231). Spatial, clustered and heterogeneous-degree networks can look similar in recorded groups. Their sample degree-correlation statistic distinguishes specified model families; the data instead support a richer model with an asymmetric relation beyond distance. The fitted latent variable has several possible interpretations. The statistic also explicitly leaves higher-order structure unidentified: exact enumeration of the two differently recurrent graphs in ANA-089 gives identical sample degree correlation (ANA-145). Anatomical nonrandomness is positive evidence about organization, but it neither uniquely determines a circuit nor establishes its semantic computation.

### Assigned roles and learned responses

The fixed assignment determines how semantic coordinates and polarities enter the population and are eventually read out. It does not require every neuron's response to every future recurrent context to be immutable. Cells with the same assigned coordinate role may participate in different learned multidimensional associations, subject to the eventual context and readout contract.

Four propositions must therefore remain separate: fixed semantic assignment, fixed geometric coordinates, fixed learned response profiles, and preserved end-to-end task behavior. A paper can preserve one while changing another. In particular, an invertible learned transform followed by its inverse can preserve the surrounding model's output while assigning a different feature basis to intermediate units. That observation does not authorize such a transform in this application, and it does not prohibit learning connections within the existing assignments.

Repeated input roles alone do not make their cells dynamically identical. In a four-cell linear example, two repeated input roles span two immediate input directions, but unequal recurrent coupling expands the span reachable through inputs over time to four; equal symmetric coupling preserves the two-dimensional span (ANA-138). [Schaub et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4961065) give precise graph-partition conditions for invariant synchronized groups under specified dynamics (LOC-221). Invariance does not establish attraction, and nonlinear group averages need not obey the same equation as a representative neuron. This supports examining learned response diversity within fixed roles without presuming either automatic diversity or automatic collapse.

[Wang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-70347-w) distinguish a cell's response classification for a particular association from a universal computational cell type (LOC-206). In their model, the same neuron can have a representation-like response for one stimulus pair and a prediction-error-like response for another. Auditory-cortex data provide indirect support: response contrasts become less correlated for dissimilar probe sounds. The experimental animals learned one expected sound, whereas the model comparison includes multiple learned pairs. The model also changes input/readout mappings and primarily analyzes static or slow input; it is not a fixed-semantic-role sequence learner. Its positive contribution is the distinction between identity, input preference and context-dependent computation, without deriving an anatomical distance from any of them.

[Hajnal et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-49845-2) find context-dependent loss of irrelevant stimulus information in mouse ACC and reproduce aspects of it with a recurrent network that infers context from previous outcomes (LOC-207). The standard network receives stimuli and reward history, not a direct context label (AUD-068). It nevertheless learns its input mappings, and the model evaluation uses the complete finite training task. Its formal geometric argument begins with linear subspace assumptions. Decoder directions depend on response/noise statistics, and smooth monotone nonlinearities can change angles (ANA-124). Fixed weights can support nonlinear context selection while input identities remain unchanged; a constant additive context offset alone cannot select between arbitrary varying inputs (ANA-125). These are useful distinctions for our future context contract, not grounds to suppress legitimate semantic dimensions or introduce a spatial arrangement.

### Context changes which existing responses are useful

Mouse interventions in [Rikhye et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0269-z) support suppression of responses associated with an irrelevant context and later reuse of the corresponding cortical representation (LOC-215). [Mukherjee et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04056-3) distinguish amplification of weak evidence from suppression of conflicting input: increasing gain can help the former and harm the latter (LOC-216). These are different computational needs. Their biological pathways do not supply a distance between our assigned semantic populations or select an application controller.

The origin of context information also matters. [Zheng et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-52289-3) demonstrate context-dependent protection, but the complex-task model routes inputs into task-disjoint groups before contextual inference (LOC-214/AUD-073). Such routing can itself carry the task identity even without a separate scalar label (ANA-134). [Hummos et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010500) combine fixed reservoir components with learned contextual effects, reward/value estimation and a change detector (LOC-217/AUD-074). The positive switching results therefore depend on the complete information pipeline. Neither study establishes that arbitrary context can be recovered from our overlapping token vectors using coactivity alone.

[Bausch et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09910-2) provide relevant human single-neuron evidence (LOC-255/AUD-099). In a picture-comparison task, many selected content responses remain invariant across questions, while other responses reinstate the task context. Entorhinal content-cell firing precedes hippocampal context-cell firing in a subset of recordings, with the association emerging during task pairing. Stable content preferences and learned contextual participation can therefore coexist. The pictures were selected to elicit recorded responses, the key lagged comparison spans six sessions, and the authors explicitly leave causal synaptic tests for future work. Their public archive contains processed results and plotting code rather than the full raw-spike analysis. These findings support the distinction between assigned role and conditional response; they do not identify a cortical minicolumn, a new synapse or a distance among semantic dimensions.

### Brain alignment does not identify a neuron layout

Topoformer's authors explicitly report similar component alignment with language-network fMRI for their non-topographic control. The spatially organized model makes those components easier to visualize on a sheet. Its controls include held-out sentences, trained versus untrained models, language versus motor regions, and an MNI-interpolation check; those controls should be retained when interpreting the result.

There is also a direct identifiability limit. For centered model responses `X` and brain responses `Y`, PLS-SVD fits the cross-covariance `X^T Y`. Permuting model features replaces this with `Q^T X^T Y`; the fitted model loadings transform by the same permutation and the component scores are unchanged. The alignment can therefore be genuine while leaving the feature layout unidentified. This exact argument concerns orthogonal/permutation changes, not every invertible transform or every regularized fitting method (ANA-040).

[Hadidi et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72253-7) additionally show that temporal splits, positional structure, word rate and regression choices materially affect LLM-to-brain comparisons on three datasets (LOC-100/AUD-020). That critique is dataset-specific: Topoformer's randomized sentence experiment is not one of those three datasets. Predictive alignment needs appropriate controls, but neither a valid alignment nor its failure directly determines physical synaptic placement.

The released human language analyses make the distinction concrete. In [Jamali et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07643-2), the displayed two-dimensional positions belong to words projected through a fitted neuronal-response representation (LOC-026/AUD-085). In [Cai et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10691-5), the compared single-unit and local-field signals share recording contacts, but the plotted contact order is sorted by response modulation (LOC-025/AUD-086). Neither inspected pipeline uses pairwise anatomical cell distances to construct its semantic map. Released split/PCA settings have specific provenance limits in the audits; those limits do not erase the separate positive evidence for semantic and contextual responses.

Mean response geometry also leaves joint activity open. An exact two-cell example preserves every cell's stimulus-conditioned response distribution and the population mean patterns, yet changing shared fluctuations changes both coactivity and joint discrimination (ANA-146). Pooled semantic tuning therefore cannot, by itself, supply the recurrent interaction statistics needed for connection learning. This does not conflict with fixed semantic roles: it distinguishes the assigned input meaning from the dynamics that must still be explained.

The ECoG study of [Morgan et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aec0518) further separates average response magnitude from linguistic association measures (LOC-253/AUD-098). Its distributed and focal functional groups derive from temporal profiles, not a supplied electrode-distance penalty. The recorded high-gamma signal is a population measurement, and the reported representational statistic is a transformed, permutation-standardized regression statistic—not information in bits or a neuronal activation fraction. This is useful evidence against treating average activity as a complete account of represented content, but it neither proves statistical independence nor maps our coordinate populations to physical locations.

### Meaningful topography and representational dimensions

Meaningful quantities can have a cortical ordering without giving an ordering to the coordinates used to encode them. [Valério et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3003820) find an anterior parietal magnitude-preference map shared by fractions and integers during active comparison of twelve symbols (LOC-120). The study includes cross-run tuning, individual maps and spatially smoothed null comparisons. It is positive evidence for abstract meaning having spatial organization, with limits: the measurements are fMRI voxels, the task explicitly requires magnitude, and the authors leave task-dependent construction or rescaling open. The two displayed response components explain **36.23%** of variance; that representation and the cortical map are distinct results. A numerical quantity corresponds to a distributed semantic pattern in our model, not to the index of one embedding coordinate.

Physical dimension, response-covariance rank and a variance-coverage count must also remain distinct. [Stringer et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1346-5) reported a broad visual-response spectrum; [Pospisil and Pillow, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2506535122) revise its estimated shape using the same recordings (LOC-121–122). In the reanalysis, roughly ten leading modes explain about **30%** of variation, while the fitted average number needed for 75% coverage is **357**. The participation ratio nevertheless increases relative to the comparison power law. Those measures answer different questions, as an exact small example demonstrates (ANA-055). Neither supplies a cortical position for each semantic dimension, and noisy neuronal estimation is not the same measurement as covariance of the complete finite Flash table.

Even a smooth response field on a one-dimensional physical circle can have arbitrarily many independent population response modes: a finite Fourier example has covariance rank twice its harmonic count (ANA-054). Thus high response rank does not conflict with low-dimensional physical support. What requires justification is the stronger demand that physical distances preserve particular response-profile distances. No such identification is assumed here.

[Tring, Duan and Ringach, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-29999-7) offer a relevant signed-channel example, with a concrete prerequisite (LOC-223). Mouse ON/OFF response domains align with retinotopic structure, and simple-cell receptive fields can be fitted by nonnegative combinations of nearby ON/OFF receptive fields. The reported sparse contributions are fitted coefficients, not directly measured individual synaptic strengths. The authors also treat mono-contrast cortical responses as proxies for thalamic input, an assumption left for direct testing. Both sensory positions and cortical positions exist before their analysis. Our positive/negative semantic populations have no corresponding retinotopic map; luminance polarity supplies neither a semantic-coordinate distance nor a general E/I classification.

[Mok and Love, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-13760-8) illustrate why a regular cognitive map need not describe neuron positions (LOC-262/AUD-103). Their clustering model learns preferred locations in an input space. Broadly sampled two-dimensional environments yield grid-like response fields, while structured concept inputs yield a different arrangement. The released learning path updates winning input preferences; it contains no recurrent neuron-distance rule. This is an algorithmic account of response organization, with biological implementation left open. Learning those preferences also differs from retaining our assigned semantic-coordinate roles.

### What the measured Flash table contributes

The [full ordinary-token covariance measurement](../research/flash-1017-full-covariance-statistics.json) uses the pinned Flash-1017 input tensor and all 127,997 ordinary token types, with uniform weight per type. Its covariance participation ratio is about **652.65**; retaining 90% of centered variance requires **3,327 principal components**. These describe different summaries of the spectrum. They do not establish a 653-dimensional replacement code or a low-dimensional anatomical map.

Statistical correlation between coordinate values is measurable without declaring coordinate numbers spatial neighbors. Conversely, a covariance-derived arrangement would be an additional modeling choice, not a physical position already contained in the table. Uniform token-type statistics also differ from frequency-weighted language experience, contextual transformer states and learned SNN activity. The measurement supplies input constraints while preserving the current 5,120-coordinate assignment.

An additional [coordinate-profile geometry calculation](../research/flash-1017-coordinate-profile-geometry.json) asks a narrower question: if each coordinate is represented by its centered, variance-normalized values across ordinary token types, can their correlation distances be represented faithfully in two dimensions? The resulting centered Gram matrix has numerical rank **5,119**. Its leading two-dimensional orthogonal projection retains **2.92% of the trace**, while retaining **30.93% of squared Gram energy**; those are different measures. This rules out an exact 2D realization of these particular distances. It does not rule out a deliberately distorted map useful for another purpose, nor show that biological positions should preserve this distance. No normalization, layout or replacement encoding is adopted (ANA-044).

The [conditional token-piece analysis](../research/flash-1017-conditional-coordinate-correlations.json) tests another limitation. With two disjoint samples of 8,192 token types per group, the strongest 16 absolute coordinate correlations overlap by **48.40%** within ASCII-letter pieces and **49.59%** within CJK-ideograph pieces, but only **4.92–5.02%** across these groups. Cross-group overlap still exceeds the independent-uniform expectation of **0.313%**. Correlations between the complete pairwise-correlation maps are about **0.93** within each group and **0.44** across groups. These are spelling categories with uniform token-type weights, not controlled semantic categories or corpus frequencies. The result establishes distribution-sensitive association in this input table; it does not select a distance, neighborhood size or physical layout (ANA-065).

The [polarity analysis](../research/flash-1017-polarity-statistics.json) separates signed coordinates from the signals their populations might receive (ANA-140). Across the same ordinary token types, a coordinate's positive-sign fraction ranges from **0.83% to 99.55%**, despite a median near 50%. Equal positive/negative population sizes therefore do not imply equal frequency of input use. For the analytical transforms `P=max(X,0)` and `N=max(-X,0)`, opposite amplitudes never overlap, but their median correlation across coordinates is **−0.442**, not −1. Complementary sign-presence indicators and halfwave amplitudes have different statistics.

For two coordinates, signed covariance combines four population covariances: `Cov(Xi,Xj) = Cov(Pi,Pj) − Cov(Pi,Nj) − Cov(Ni,Pj) + Cov(Ni,Nj)`. The strongest raw coordinate pair has correlation **+0.501**, while its absolute amplitudes have correlation **−0.498**. Thus raw signed correlation cannot replace a particular population-pair coactivity measurement. These are input-table transforms, not a chosen activation function, measured neuron activity, corpus-frequency statistics or semantic-valence labels. They introduce no allocation change, normalization rule or distance.

Preserving raw vector angles is also insufficient to preserve signed-population overlap. For the analytical halfwave transform `H(x)=(max(x,0),max(-x,0))`, the identity `H(x)·H(y)=(x·y+|x|·|y|)/2` shows the extra dependence on the assigned basis. A 45-degree rotation of two orthogonal unit vectors preserves their raw inner product but changes this halfwave overlap from zero to one half (ANA-163). This is a representation counterexample, not a selected encoder: an equivalent raw-vector similarity does not authorize reassigning the population roles to fit a convenient map.

The [complete halfwave-profile calculation](../research/flash-1017-full-polarity-statistics.json) covers all 10,240 positive/negative amplitude channels (ANA-147). Their covariance has full numerical rank and participation ratio **756.49**; 952 components retain half its variance. For their centered, variance-normalized profile Gram matrix, a rank-two spectral projection retains **2.11% of trace** and **29.27% of squared Gram energy**. This remains a global preservation diagnostic, with the same limitation as the raw-coordinate calculation: it neither requires nor rules out a useful physical sheet.

The strongest 16 positive halfwave-profile correlations, mapped back to coordinate identities, overlap by **52.21%** with the corresponding raw-coordinate absolute-correlation list. Both representation and the ranking sign convention differ in this comparison; it does not isolate either change. The resulting frequency with which a profile appears in these lists is a descriptive ranking statistic, not a live degree or biological scale law.

The [polarity distribution-sensitivity calculation](../research/flash-1017-polarity-distribution-sensitivity.json) uses the same six disjoint 8,192-token cohorts as the raw-coordinate analysis (ANA-148). Within-group positive-partner overlap is **36.86%** for ASCII pieces and **38.56%** for CJK pieces, versus **4.37–4.44%** across groups. Cross-group overlap remains above the independent-uniform baseline of **0.156%**. Thus population-pair input associations depend on the sampling distribution as well as the representation. Probabilistic assignment to the sixty cells, recurrent context and time-dependent coactivity still require their own definitions.

The [local-neighborhood projection check](../research/flash-1017-profile-projection-neighborhoods.json) addresses a different question from global rank: how many of each halfwave profile's strongest 16 partners survive an exact spectral projection (ANA-159)? Mean overlap is **0.53% in 1D**, **0.96% in 2D**, **8.65% in 32D** and **56.86% in 2,048D**; independently uniform partner lists would overlap by **0.156%**. In 2D, selected partners have mean original correlation **0.0365**, compared with **0.1038** for the true strongest partners. These results argue against treating this simple projection as a faithful neighborhood map. They do not test every nonlinear map, choose correlation as a learning score, or turn input profiles into measured activity of the sixty individual cells. The objective is global Gram approximation, so local-neighbor accuracy need not improve monotonically with projection dimension.

### Nonlinear maps and what their neighbors mean

A [nonlinear 2D countercheck](../research/flash-1017-nonlinear-profile-map.json) uses all 10,240 halfwave profiles, the full pairwise t-SNE objective and two random starts, with no PCA preprocessing (ANA-168; method reference [van der Maaten and Hinton, 2008](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume9/vandermaaten08a/vandermaaten08a.pdf)). At perplexity 30 after 1,000 updates, the two maps retain **10.01% and 9.99%** of each profile's original top-16 partners. Selected partners have mean original correlation about **0.0502**, versus **0.1038** for the strongest partners and **0.0158** for uniformly chosen eligible partners. The maps therefore retain a meaningful descriptive association above this baseline, despite losing many exact nearest partners; no semantic-task benefit is inferred. This improves substantially on the spectral 2D projection, so its loss cannot be generalized to every nonlinear map.

The two map-neighbor lists overlap by **38.18%** at size 16. Similar objective scores therefore do not identify a unique neighbor assignment. A float64 direct-distance check leaves both reported recall means unchanged. Two starts, one perplexity and a finite optimization run do not establish the best possible map; neither low recall nor a changed ranking is by itself a semantic-task error. The diagnostic has one point per coordinate-polarity channel, not sixty individually trained cells; its limitations do not constrain every possible cell-level organization. These coordinates are analytical outputs, not adopted neuron positions.

There is also a separate, limited mathematical obstruction to perfect preservation. In a strict Euclidean closest-`k` graph with distinct positions and one excluded opponent per profile, a point can be selected by at most `2(k+1)` profiles on a line or `6(k+1)` in a plane. The [cone argument and measured counts](../research/flash-1017-neighborhood-realization-bound.json) give top-16 recall upper bounds of **69.86% for 1D** and **90.69% for 2D** (ANA-166). These bounds are loose and do not explain the full optimizer loss. They concern this constant-size neighbor diagnostic, not a variable-degree probabilistic connection rule, a periodic surface or our capacity classes. [Schibler et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.SoCG.2025.73) provide the broader distinction between ordinal neighbor realization and metric preservation (LOC-267).

High ranking popularity also needs its own interpretation. [Radovanović et al., 2010](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume11/radovanovic10a/radovanovic10a.pdf) show how distribution and metric can create nearest-neighbor hubs, with counterexamples to a universal dimensionality effect (LOC-268). In the actual profiles, popularity and average correlation have a **Spearman association of 0.467** for the top-16 lists ([centrality calculation](../research/flash-1017-profile-centrality.json), ANA-167). This is a partial descriptive association, not a causal explanation or evidence that the popular profiles should become biological-style hub neurons. No hub suppression, mean removal or replacement capacity law follows.

### What counts as coactivity

The [association-metric comparison](../research/flash-1017-association-metric-sensitivity.json) demonstrates that the input table does not determine a unique functional neighborhood (ANA-164). For the same 10,240 halfwave profiles and uniform ordinary-token sample, mean overlap with each profile's strongest 16 Pearson-correlated partners is **1.01%** when ranking by raw expected amplitude product, **7.55%** with uncentered amplitude cosine, and **61.07%** with covariance. One profile appears in every eligible list under the raw-product ranking. That is a descriptive ranking concentration, not an inferred biological degree or a selected graph.

Raw products retain the contribution from the two means; covariance subtracts it, and Pearson correlation also normalizes variance. The mean contribution need not be unwanted: the learning objective must determine what is useful. Nor are these population-amplitude statistics the observed coactivity of the sixty individual cells, which depends on their eventual sampling and recurrent dynamics. Thus a correlation-derived layout would add both a statistical definition and a geometric constraint; neither follows from the words “fire together.”

This distinction has a long theoretical history. [Sejnowski, 1977](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00275079) separates covariance storage from uncorrected coincidences under explicit small-change and stochastic-model assumptions (LOC-264). [Gütig et al., 2003](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-09-03697.2003) show that weight dependence and temporal filtering affect how input relationships enter learned weights (LOC-265). Their sensitivity measure concerns the learned weight distribution, with readout behavior explicitly left outside it. Neither study supplies a physical metric or selects the correct statistic for our semantic regimes.

### Physical opportunity and functional selection

[Markov et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhq201) directly measure highly local intrinsic inputs in macaque cortex, including an approximately exponential decline of **labeled-source density** around small tracer injections (LOC-178). Their fraction of labeled neurons is not a fraction of all possible source–target pairs. A labeled source can contact any of several targets in the uptake zone; available cell density, uptake extent and detection affect the measured profile. Under an illustrative independent-contact model, labeling probability is `1 − product(1 − p_ij)`, which differs from any individual pair probability (ANA-099). The finding supports local anatomical investment without identifying a pairwise kernel, its amplitude or a distance between semantic coordinates.

[Cossell et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14182) support a distinction within a local cortical population: response similarity predicts excitatory connection strength better than cortical distance, and rare strong connections carry a large share of measured excitation (LOC-116). Their input model shows how feature-matched strong inputs can dominate selective subthreshold modulation. It sums stimulus-generated responses into one target per simulation; it does not close the recurrent loop. The local sample and weak connections' possible contextual/plasticity roles also limit any inference that only strong inputs matter. [Matelsky et al.'s 2026 preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.08.723866) distinguishes segmentation-derived cell contacts from synaptic partnerships (LOC-115). Its whole-arbor neighborhoods are not soma-centered distance neighborhoods, and its graph-density denominators need clarification before numerical reuse. These sources distinguish opportunity from selected influence without supplying an application contact graph.

A proposed functional distance derived entirely from the coactivity statistic already used for learning may simply reweight that same statistic. Under that condition, an additional distance factor does not add independent anatomical evidence (ANA-053). Reweighting could still be useful, but its benefit must be identified as such. Fixed semantic roles permit learned response relationships without making those relationships physical positions.

[Reimann et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2026.116144) find that a confirmed connection predicts additional connections near its anatomical target beyond a heterogeneous-degree control (LOC-140). Their geometric spread model reproduces several higher-order graph statistics. This is evidence about dependencies among edges, beyond an independent distance-decay probability. Variable actual degree also creates nonspatial edge associations (ANA-071); allocated capacity must not be substituted for actual degree. The study does not measure a recurrent task benefit, and its released generator differs from the printed candidate-exclusion rule (AUD-033/ANA-073). The anatomical finding remains useful, while exact algorithm reuse requires reconciliation.

The Campagnola software audit illustrates why a reported distance correction needs its own provenance (LOC-219/AUD-077). Its documented approximation divides observed connection fraction by the mean of an assumed Gaussian profile over tested distances. An incorrect assumed width can produce different corrected amplitudes from the same underlying circuit sampled at different distances (ANA-137). The inspected release also distinguishes a freely fitted profile, fixed-width fitting and a combined correction pipeline; one pipeline stores a width in a sibling dictionary entry that its fitter does not read. That version-specific discrepancy is not evidence that every published analysis used the affected path. The article's complete methods remain an access gap in this review, and a precise cross-species width inference is withheld.

### Morphology, selective contacts and the sampling reference

[Kanari et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2025.112928) provide a useful positive control: placing length-scaled mouse dendrites within a human-like reconstruction does not recover the human pyramidal network's directed-clique structure. Branch organization contributes beyond dendritic length and neuron placement (LOC-184). The reconstruction nevertheless supplies anatomical positions, repairs incomplete axons and prunes possible contacts toward a chosen density. Its released Figure 3 notebook, saved outputs and published panels do not fully agree on degree distributions, density and logarithm labeling; exact graph statistics remain unreconciled (AUD-049). The qualitative hybrid control is retained. Large ordered cliques can be entirely feedforward, so their size alone does not establish recurrent circulation or memory (ANA-104).

[Barros-Zulaica et al.'s 2026 preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.02.12.705493) separates contact opportunities from several ways of selecting surviving edges (LOC-185). Matching the number of contacts, the number of connected partners, degree distributions or distance dependence controls different quantities. Its cooperatively pruned model preserves more anatomical structure than independent contact removal, and relocating reciprocal connections changes higher-order structure despite preserving their count. These are positive structural controls, with supplied morphology and positions. Some biological quantities calibrate the pruning model; human temporal and juvenile rat somatosensory data also differ in age and region. Neither a causal learning rule nor useful semantic recurrence is established by those endpoint statistics.

The distinction between pooled enrichment and a conditioned comparison is visible in an exploratory audit of the public [Planert data](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.28184126.v1), which share their experimental origin with the Peng recordings (AUD-050). Neighbors are chosen from complete-distance recorded clusters before inspecting outcomes; repeated conflicting outcomes are excluded. The reference preserves each source's tested targets and outgoing count. Additional distance conditioning preserves that count within 50-micrometer source-to-target distance bins.

| Recorded subset | Jointly connected target/nearest-neighbor opportunities | Expected with source outgoing counts fixed | Expected with source outgoing counts and distance bins fixed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Four known e-types: 2,954 opportunities | 87 | 87.79 | 89.38 |
| Broader pyramidal sample: 4,875 opportunities | 189 | 164.05 | 179.54 |

In the known-e-type subset, the pooled target connection rate rises from 14.05% to 19.55% when its nearest recorded neighbor is connected, despite no positive excess over the source-count reference. The broader sample retains a residual that decreases after distance conditioning. These results support neither a universal absence of locality nor an unconditional neighbor-learning rule. The calculation is a secondary analysis of a different curated subset, not a reproduction of the papers' offset-conditioned analyses. Its exact finite-sampling reference does not preserve target indegrees or supply participant-level inference; narrow strata leave few alternatives (ANA-106). A nearest recorded cell is also not the nearest anatomical neuron.

An increasing chance of another contact among pairs already having many contacts need not identify cooperative growth: a mixture of fixed pair propensities produces that pattern through selection alone (ANA-107). Similarly, anatomical contacts and five-byte connection slots need not correspond one-to-one. Multiple contacts collapse exactly to one effective weight only under the relevant linear, common-delay and common-filter assumptions; heterogeneous delays or separate nonlinear integration can prevent that equivalence (ANA-108). These are interpretation boundaries for the existing compact representation, not a requirement to add contact, delay or compartment fields.

### Functional organization and inferred wiring

[Jiang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72510-9) relate activity-derived hubs and modules to visual encoding and learning (LOC-132). [Cai et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/nsr/nwag420) recover meaningful functional groups from activity without supplying anatomy to the clustering algorithm (LOC-133). These are useful functional organizations, but their edges or memberships are not direct synapse measurements. In particular, changing a functional group label does not require changing a cell's assigned semantic identity. The FRID source uses ridge-regularized anchor coefficients and their magnitudes for clustering; neither the regularizer nor the magnitude operation establishes sparse signed synaptic connectivity (AUD-030).

Connectivity reconstruction has conditional successes and failure modes. [Laasch et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-88596-y) find that different estimators work best for different dynamical and delay regimes (LOC-137). Exact examples also show that zero-lag partial correlation can miss directly coupled pairs while retaining an indirect pair, even with full observation and unlimited data (ANA-069). These inference limits do not invalidate learning from coactivity. They mean that a useful learning objective and a claim of anatomical reconstruction require different evidence.

### Functional patterns across spontaneous and driven states

[Rózsa et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68578-y) model prescribed orientation preferences together with functionally biased recurrence, accounting for several spontaneous, visual and optogenetic phenomena (LOC-199). Some parameter comparisons match mean activity by adjusting external drive, so the results should not be dismissed as merely different average firing levels. Functional preference, spatial reach and conduction delay nevertheless remain separate supplied ingredients. The positive analogy is that fixed input roles can coexist with organized recurrent responses across regimes. An orientation map provides meaningful feature relations; embedding-coordinate numbers do not inherit those relations. The released model is not a long-term connection-learning algorithm (AUD-062).

[Inácio et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08631-w) trace inputs to individual movement-correlated and uncorrelated cortical neurons (LOC-200). Their tested local input distributions are similar, while distant input-source composition differs. Pathway suppression supports a substantial thalamic contribution to movement-related activity. The study therefore distinguishes functional input identity from coarse local spread. Its null comparisons do not prove anatomical equivalence, and distances among presynaptic cells do not define a source-to-target wiring probability. The source analysis also centers input clouds and normalizes transport cost; that statistic is not a physical connection kernel (AUD-063).

[Mulholland et al., 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.03.31.715687) stimulate developing ferret cortex with patterns derived from spontaneous activity and compare them with random patterns of matched spatial wavelength (LOC-201). Endogenous patterns yield greater trial reliability and stimulus-response fidelity. Their average peak amplitude is not significantly greater in the main comparison. This is positive evidence that circuit-compatible pattern structure matters beyond spatial scale. More stable responses during five seconds of stimulation do not demonstrate recurrence after the drive stops. The model's ordinary trials use static spatial-noise realizations; sustained trials add temporally correlated noise. Those protocols must remain distinct from the application's still-unspecified DMN drive.

Spontaneous covariance is also not a general recipe for selecting optimal inputs. With static isotropic input and linear response `y = R*eta`, covariance is proportional to `R*R^T`: its modes describe output patterns, whereas maximal input gain follows the right singular vectors of `R`. Symmetric positive response operators align these modes, providing a useful special case. Stable nonsymmetric examples can have identical spontaneous covariance and different preferred inputs (ANA-120). The preprint tests its predicted patterns directly; its result should be preserved without promoting the covariance proxy into an unconfirmed partner-selection rule.

Human MTL recordings in [Umbach et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-33587-0) relate assembly firing order and changing cell participation to episodic recall (LOC-261). The spatial evidence is mixed across definitions: pooled assembly-member pairs more often share a recording channel, region or hemisphere, while an aggregate score against within-session random groups is not significant. Both findings matter. They support neither a claim of no anatomical organization nor a calibrated distance law for semantic features. Coactivity is also explicitly distinguished from measured physical connections. The reported negative semantic test groups words by similarity to one reference word; that projection cannot exclude all multidimensional semantic tuning (ANA-161). These findings concern flexible participation and sampled activity, without prescribing our cell identities, phases or oscillation frequencies.

### Causal influence and functional completion

[Oldenburg et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01510-5) causally manipulate cortical ensembles and find that spatial compactness and feature coherence jointly affect nearby activation and broader suppression (LOC-129). Their controls address optical spread, stimulation strength and visual context. The critical compact co-tuned subgroup nevertheless contains eight ensembles from one mouse, within a larger thirteen-mouse dataset. Its replication must be stated at the relevant level. Optical off-target exclusions are measurement precautions, not evidence for a forbidden near-neighbor wiring zone.

[Chau, Miller and Palmigiano, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2426758122) show how recurrent excitation, inhibition and neuronal gain can change influence range and feature dependence without changing anatomical connection widths (LOC-131). Their exact theory covers stable strong coupling for which a sum over recurrent paths does not converge, and the released work includes sparse controls. The fitted circuit is constrained to be inhibition-stabilized; that property is not an unconstrained discovery. Observing even every excitatory-to-excitatory steady response need not identify direct excitation separately from hidden inhibitory paths (ANA-064/066). Thus an experimentally measured influence kernel cannot simply become our direct connection-selection kernel.

[Shin et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02055-5) provide positive evidence for functional pattern completion: stimulating illusory-contour-selective neurons recruits decodable contour-related activity in unstimulated cells, whereas matched segment-responsive groups do not produce the same local result (LOC-130). The main local experiment uses a one-second stimulation train; its decoder result is measured during that drive. A separate analysis removes features from a decoder rather than silencing neurons. Neither demonstrates an autonomous semantic loop, but the causal recruitment result is relevant to defining a future functional objective independently of any assumed layout.

Human language recordings require the same separation of evidence. [The 2026 single-neuron language study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10691-5) predicts neural responses with contextual representations and sentence-held-out regression, and finds differences between single-unit and local-field selectivity at recording contacts (LOC-025). Its figure 5 orders contacts by response modulation, not physical position. The findings support heterogeneous local representation; they neither establish a semantic-coordinate tissue map nor demonstrate that local functional organization is absent.

[Carrillo-Reid et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.05.045) show that a few functionally selected cortical cells can recruit a larger ensemble and improve visual-task behavior (LOC-144). The functional model helps choose intervention targets without being a synapse map. The strong result uses a weak visual cue; without cues, full recall occurs in only about 5% of photostimulation trials, and the larger behavioral success rate is conditional on those successful recalls. [Geiller et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04169-9) likewise demonstrate local recruitment after inducing a place field in one hippocampal cell (LOC-142). Represented belt location, soma separation and functional membership remain distinct. These experiments establish useful cooperation and its context dependence, not a distance between semantic dimensions.

### Propagation, synchrony and persistent activity

[Betzel et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcsy.0000091) demonstrate modality-specific propagation and convergence on the adult fly connectome (LOC-112). Their model contains active, inactive and refractory states, but a neuron becomes permanently refractory for each cascade. It can fire only once. Consequently, the sum of active fractions over all cascade steps is at most one: a positive steady fraction cannot persist indefinitely, even if the graph contains cycles (ANA-048). These states cannot be identified with our inactive, predictive and active phases. Joint seeding also doubles the initial population; earlier mean arrival can result from multiple independent opportunities without a nonlinear cooperative interaction (ANA-047).

[Luppi et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02205-3) provide a stronger computational comparison: signed effective interaction graphs improve both several brain-dynamics measures and a separate reservoir memory task (LOC-113). The controls include split-half fitting, greater positive-only parameter range/density, regularization and topology randomizations. The human memory score improves from **4.27 to 4.35**, about **1.9%**; this modest task effect is distinct from the much larger improvement in fitting functional connectivity. The score sums absolute correlations across delays, and the best score is selected across sixteen spectral radii. It is a driven recall benchmark, not autonomous semantic replay.

Their primary generative model uses two-variable Hopf oscillators with signed **diffusive coupling**, `g * (z_other - z_self)`. This term changes both the cross-node influence and the self term. In the conditional two-node case with identical oscillators, the opposing mode has linear growth `a - 2g`; sufficiently negative `g` can activate that mode, while cubic saturation bounds its amplitude. Reducing synchrony therefore need not mean reducing every activity amplitude. The oscillator's internal rotation is essential to this example; a signed one-variable neuronal rule does not inherit its behavior (ANA-049).

The distinction also applies to fitted weights. [Tanner et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50248-6) explicitly distinguish negative regression coefficients from inhibitory synapses and explanatory one-step prediction from a generative model (LOC-110). Coordinate polarity, semantic valence, effective suppression, synchronization and active-neuron occupancy remain separate quantities. A mechanism must eventually establish the particular semantic trajectory and activity behavior we require; a favorable result on another quantity is supporting evidence, not that acceptance result.

Global activity control is also insufficient to characterize recurrent modes. [Muir and Mrsic-Flogel, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.91.042808) analyze how modular or confined coupling changes mode stability despite controlled total strength (LOC-117). In a separate exact example, shared inhibitory feedback stabilizes the population mean while a difference between two groups grows (ANA-051). [Li and Shew, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008268) and [Barreiro et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13408-017-0052-6) further distinguish activity levels, synchrony and nonlinear periodic behavior (LOC-118–119). Their quantitative assumptions and released-code limitations are recorded in the evidence ledger. These results establish neither a selected controller nor meaningful semantic recurrence merely from bounded activity or oscillation.

Observed coordination also does not uniquely recover its supporting wiring. [Gili et al.'s 2024 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02674v1) fits balanced graph partitions to group-level phase-locking patterns from eleven language-related regions (LOC-222). Phase-locking value can equal one for signals with different amplitudes and opposite instantaneous signs. A separate four-node enumeration admits eight equally minimal graph edits supporting the same prescribed partition; this is a general non-uniqueness example, not a refutation of the preprint's particular eleven-region solutions (ANA-139). Phase coordination, invariant synchronization, attractive dynamics and a unique anatomical explanation remain separate claims.

### Triggering a pattern and controlling its spread

[Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11174-3) distinguish representing a learned movement from triggering its population dynamics (LOC-235). Stimulating early-onset motor ensembles can evoke learned patterns and movements; late-onset ensembles do not have the same effect. Initial activity and task engagement influence success. Anatomical controls detect no early/late differences in the tested location and nearest-neighbor measures, which is narrower than demonstrating spatial equivalence. Learned activity can occur without movement, separating internal pattern generation from behavioral output. The final abstract/captions and archived plotting outputs support these distinctions; the full final methods and end-to-end analysis were not reviewed (AUD-090).

[Yamamoto et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.ade1755) provide a controlled positive case for modular organization (LOC-236). Asynchronous stimulation reduces network-wide bursting more effectively in strongly modular cortical cultures. Their spiking comparisons adjust mean incoming degree to approximately 30 across topologies, while resource depletion makes the few intermodule pathways more sensitive to stimulation. A coarse model requires stochastic resource-dependent communication gates. Mean firing, burst size and synchrony remain separate measurements.

In the inspected source, geometry constructs adjacency; subsequent neuronal equations use the resulting graph and internal states without a distance-dependent propagation term (AUD-091). The computational effect can therefore reside in graph organization rather than an explicit physical coordinate during execution. This does not determine which graph serves our application. The four prescribed 40-cell modules, inhibitory population and extra resource states are not our minicolumns or adopted mechanisms, and the study does not demonstrate learned semantic cycling.

[Wright et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42229417/) report a causal contribution of plasticity within motor cortex to learned population dynamics (LOC-237; published abstract reviewed). Here, local identifies the manipulated area/cells, not a measured distance kernel. Their released activity-association analysis does not assay direct anatomical edges (AUD-092). Full methods remain necessary before attributing the effect specifically to recurrent synapses rather than other inputs onto those cells.

### Mechanisms of persistent and evolving responses

[Chaudhuri et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-019-0460-x) identify a ring in head-direction population activity across waking and sleep (LOC-047/AUD-087). This is a response manifold, regardless of whether the cells possess a corresponding anatomical topography. Sleep persistence supports an internally maintained brain representation; the authors leave open whether the mechanism is confined to the recorded nucleus or involves other regions. Their sleep dynamics also involve inferred velocity and global-drive signals. Our immersion requirement must therefore state what background drive is permitted; zero background input is not an existing decision.

[Vinograd et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07915-x) provide causal evidence for selective persistent integration in hypothalamic ensembles (LOC-232). Targeted stimulation along an integration mode accumulates activity; stimulation of a different group produces transient displacement followed by return. Selective influence on follower cells survives exclusion of nearby cells that might receive light spillover. Their linear spatial classifier does not distinguish the groups, and the measured influence-versus-distance regression is nonsignificant. These findings support functional organization without requiring simply separated anatomical parcels. They do not exclude every spatial relationship, and the optical exclusion radius is not a proposed very-near wiring exemption.

[Mountoufaris et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.08.015) show that disrupting two peptide receptors reduces the slow integration mode and persistent responses while leaving some immediate behavioral decoding relatively preserved (LOC-233). Temporal maintenance is therefore a distinct property from response selectivity or overall activity. The peptide sources are extrinsic to the nucleus, and the study leaves several cellular mechanisms open. This is evidence about biological persistence, not a justification for a semantic-valence bias or a selected additional state variable.

[Kennedy et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2728-4) offer a positive locality comparison (LOC-234). Their tested models preserve both evolving activity and stimulus distinctions using either longer slow currents or a combination of weak one-dimensional connection bias and spatially structured inputs. Stronger segregation fails to match the measured partial overlap. The useful effect is preservation of stimulus distinctions during persistence; the source supplies the input alignment and index ordering that make its spatial variant work. It establishes neither a 2D advantage nor an ordering for our fixed semantic coordinates. The inspected author manuscript and later source have parameter/normalization differences, and the released spatial comparison changes input statistics as well as recurrence (AUD-089/ANA-150).

Persistence assessment must retain the direction of state changes. The plotted three-dimensional surface in the referenced visualization notebook is speed magnitude, not an energy potential (AUD-088). Exact linear examples give the same trough-shaped speed surface with attraction, repulsion or shear; similarly, a long absolute characteristic time can describe decay or growth (ANA-149). The biological studies also examine vector fields and interventions. For our application, these distinctions support checking actual semantic trajectories and recovery, alongside the permitted broad per-CC activity band.

### Computational benefits of functional grouping

[Carrillo-Reid et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf7560) add a relevant spontaneous-activity result: repeatedly coactivated cortical ensembles later recur without stimulation and can remain available the next day, alongside existing visual ensembles (LOC-146). [Mocle et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2024.02.007) show that excitability and pre-existing functional groups influence which hippocampal cells join a memory-supporting ensemble; subsequent silencing provides causal evidence for their contribution (LOC-149). Learned cooperative participation is therefore a useful research target within existing roles. Neither study equates an assembly with a minicolumn, establishes continuous autonomous cycling, or supplies the missing physical metric.

[Cano and Kempter, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013844) demonstrate that recurrent excitation within an activity assembly can amplify weak feedforward input and sustain sequence propagation (LOC-138). The mechanism works in tested networks with different inhibitory arrangements, including a suitable excitatory-only regime. Its success depends on the membrane-state distribution, leak and synaptic timing. The main test is a triggered passage through ten predefined, non-overlapping groups under baseline input; connections are prescribed, and the final group has no feedforward wraparound. The analytical population approximation permits each cell to contribute only once. Longer open-chain replay therefore does not establish repeated autonomous cycling or determine a per-CC active-fraction band (AUD-032/ANA-070).

[Ecker et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.101850.3) provide a different benefit: calcium-dependent plasticity improves the reliability and specificity of driven assembly sequences while single-neuron reliability slightly decreases (LOC-139). Their dendritic clusters can form stable within-assembly support or participate in cross-assembly changes; clustered synapses are not uniformly the most plastic. The detailed model and selected MICrONS comparisons support distinct roles for joint input, dendritic placement and network structure. Dendritic compartments and calcium dynamics are additional computational assumptions, not a distance metric between our semantic-role cells.

[Jones et al., 2026, preprint v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24503v1) give a controlled task example: learned sparse inputs to nonlinear dendritic subunits improve parameter efficiency on covariance discrimination relative to the compared MLPs (LOC-141). Input identities remain fixed while branch membership is learned under a synapse budget. The comparison includes held-out samples and parameter/depth controls, but uses backpropagation and a feedforward classifier. Shuffling learned placement also changes properties beyond clustering itself. Its conditional variance argument explains why grouping correlated inputs can help a joint nonlinear computation; it does not establish a natural distance between input labels, a Hebbian training rule or autonomous recurrence (ANA-072).

These results distinguish useful amplification, reliable group responses and joint-input computation. An assembly can span semantic dimensions; it is not identified with a minicolumn by these studies. Any transfer would need to specify its functional objective and required state within the current semantic organization. No assembly partition, dendritic field, distance or learning mechanism is selected here.

Duration is a separate functional quantity. [Fernández-Ruiz et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax0758) causally prolong ongoing hippocampal ripples and improve memory-dependent behavior, with delayed-stimulation controls; interrupting events impairs performance (LOC-145). [Vancura et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01306-7) find subtype-specific inhibitory activity associated with event duration (LOC-143), but do not establish that association by a causal subtype intervention. Appropriate timing and additional participating cells matter; neither result says that unlimited persistence is better or selects an activity controller for the CC.

[Johnston and Fusi's reviewed 2024 preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11482777/) shows how context-specific groups and subspaces can emerge without anatomical constraints (LOC-259). Their usefulness depends on the input and on whether future contexts reuse learned decision rules. This supports studying functional separation and sharing directly. The network learns its hidden roles and receives explicit context; neither the existence of 5,120 coordinates nor the Flash covariance spectrum identifies its task-relevant latent dimension. The source was extensively revised in January 2026 under the title *When and why modular representations emerge*; only that revision's metadata and abstract were available, so detailed findings here remain attributed to the inspected 2024 version.

### Learning cooperation within assigned input roles

[Eckmann et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2305326121) provide a particularly relevant positive control: in Figure 5, feedforward tuning stays fixed while competitive Hebbian learning changes recurrent weights, producing contrast normalization and winner-take-all behavior (LOC-186). Thus their entire result must not be dismissed as learning new input identities. Other experiments in the same paper do learn feedforward receptive fields. The fixed-input case uses dense continuous-rate excitatory/inhibitory populations, separate synapse-type normalization and strong training assumptions. Its center/surround extension supplies two sensory regions; it does not infer physical coordinates from arbitrary feature indices. The released code supports freezing feedforward weights, but its default is plastic, and the inspected release is not an identified reproduction of the published Figure 5 (AUD-051). This is evidence that recurrent cooperation can be learned around assigned roles, not proof that winner-take-all is suitable for our valid multidimensional vectors or autonomous regimes.

The normalization target matters. For `n` equal positive inputs, an L1 weight budget can preserve mean drive while reducing independent fluctuations; an L2 budget can preserve independent fluctuation variance while mean drive grows as `sqrt(n)`. Shared input correlation changes both conclusions (ANA-109). Neither normalization is automatically an activity-stability guarantee. These are training and integration choices, distinct from the static allocation of connection slots.

[Delamare et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0846-23.2024) provide a constructive model in which fixed preferred inputs coexist with learned recurrent assemblies and transient excitability-dependent allocation (LOC-150). Nonpreferred inputs remain nonzero, and inhibition regulates competition. Related experiences can recruit overlapping cells when separated by a suitable interval; partial cues can reactivate an assembly. This supports distinguishing fixed input roles from fixed learned responses. The model uses dense rate-network plasticity and additional excitability state; its context groups are not individual signed coordinates. Some simulations are excluded for excessive firing, and memories generally remain silent without a cue. It therefore supplies neither an unconditional stability result nor an autonomous semantic cycle. Released code also differs from some printed parameters and protocols (AUD-037).

[Asabuki and Fukai, 2025](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.92712.3) show how predictive plasticity can make spontaneous **population firing mass** reflect experienced stimulus probabilities (LOC-151). Inhibitory competition and adaptive excitability participate in the result; an extension respects separate excitatory and inhibitory populations. Learned feedforward selectivity differs from our assigned semantic roles. Ordered training does not yield ordered replay, and an assembly's accumulated firing is not its number of visits: event frequency, event duration, participating cells and background firing all contribute (ANA-076). The released input protocol and transfer function require reconciliation with the published equations before quantitative reproduction; a direct bound on the literal printed sigmoid would make its illustrated activity implausible (AUD-036/ANA-077). These issues do not erase the demonstrated computational distinction between a learned activity distribution and a learned sequence law.

[Zemliak et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013304) add a direct fixed-input example: one-to-one input identities coexist with recurrent Hebbian learning that distinguishes familiar from novel patterns (LOC-249/AUD-095). This strengthens the case that assigned roles need not prevent learned cooperation. The observation is stimulus-driven familiarity, with optimized plasticity and classification thresholds, all-to-all excitation and weight normalization. Known stimulated cells provide the readout; the released update also differs from the printed trace formula. This does not demonstrate autonomous recall of the pattern's content or an ordered semantic cycle.

### Context-sensitive sequence learning and cyclic recall

[Deveau et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2025.12.024) find that the same visual input can recruit different responses according to preceding context, and that the incremental effect of optogenetic input depends on the surrounding natural sequence (LOC-196). Their recurrent model keeps input/output mappings fixed while learning recurrence. This is directly relevant to distinguishing assigned input roles from context-sensitive participation. The released example uses disjoint stimulus patterns and differs from parts of the final printed training protocol, so it does not establish performance on our overlapping dense vectors (AUD-059). A linear, time-invariant additive circuit can retain history in its total response but cannot make the incremental response to the same added input depend on that history (ANA-118); state-dependent interactions are a substantive part of this result.

[Eom et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-026-10064-8) combine hippocampal experiments with a model in which input-specific excitability temporarily recruits prior-event cells into subsequent events, enabling sequence association (LOC-197). This offers a concrete account of context-sensitive participation without moving cell identities. The model supplies random connections, strong event inputs, dendritic state and different encoding/recall settings; its time constants are compressed for simulation. Released source also normalizes incoming weights and cues the first event (AUD-060). Successful one-pass recall is not evidence of indefinite semantic cycling, and its excitability variables do not fit into the existing scalar connection weight without a separate state design.

[Klampfl and Maass, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5044-12.2013) demonstrate learned temporal assemblies, spontaneous partial replay and cue completion (LOC-176). Their fixed-random-input control preserves sequence learning; a single large competitive circuit also produces sequences. The main construction nevertheless imposes a 2D grid, and neither control is a matched geometry-only comparison. Longer shared inputs can lose context specificity. Learned specialization also reduces performance on their generic XOR and fading-memory tasks. These results support flexible recurrent responses under fixed inputs while showing that more stereotyped replay is not an unconditional benefit.

[Papadimitriou et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2001893117) demonstrate assembly projection, association, completion and merging in random graphs without a physical metric (LOC-177). Their model selects exactly the top `k` excitatory winners and assumes control primitives and plasticity beyond coactivity alone. A stabilizing assembly support is not an autonomous semantic cycle. The released simulator generates unseen candidates statistically and expands the seen support (AUD-046). Its exchangeability assumption can remain valid with fixed labels if those labels do not affect candidate scores; role-dependent drive or learned statistics require a separate check (ANA-100). This neither chooses a sampler nor changes our static-pool design.

[Asabuki and Clopath, 2025](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.95243.3) learn stochastic transitions among predefined stimulus groups with prediction-based excitatory and inhibitory plasticity (LOC-152). After training, spontaneous activity under background drive reproduces aspects of first-order transition structure. The result is relevant to assigned roles, but whole-stimulus groups differ from overlapping tokens represented across shared coordinate populations. Input duration is not reproduced, and the authors identify limitations for higher-order context and temporal gaps. Their mixed-selectivity extension concerns inhibitory cells, not arbitrary overlapping excitatory encodings. Released transition analysis conditions on accepted event pairs and uses a narrower gap rule than the paper; conditional accuracy must be distinguished from event coverage, overlaps and timing (AUD-038/ANA-078).

[Kappel, Nessler and Maass, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003511) offer an earlier stochastic sequence model based on spike-timing plasticity and competition (LOC-154). Different cells can represent the same current stimulus in different recent contexts, supporting history-sensitive prediction and short cue-triggered completion. This is a computational example of fixed observable meaning coexisting with variable internal state; it does not assign physical coordinates. Both input and recurrent weights are learned, and demanding context tasks additionally use repeated presentation and a modulatory rejection procedure. Constant total conditional firing intensity in the theoretical winner-take-all circuit is not a demonstrated per-CC phase-fraction controller. A first-order law for complete neural state need not imply a first-order law for semantic observations: merging states with different successor distributions introduces observable history dependence (ANA-081).

[Brea, Senn and Pfister, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4098-12.2013) supply positive evidence for **learned cyclic recall**, including repeated simple binary patterns without further initial clamping (LOC-155). Visible cells carry a prescribed training sequence; additional hidden activity can support context and improve learning. The learning objective matches spontaneous and taught sequence distributions, and hidden-cell plasticity requires a global or semiglobal factor whose suggested biological implementation is hypothetical. Cyclic success in the simple examples must be separated from the paper's cue-initialized precise-spike examples, where errors can accumulate. Its visible-only capacity result assumes a restricted threshold model and is not a capacity estimate for this application. Internal memory is a functional requirement for ambiguous histories; dedicated hidden neurons are one possible realization, not an adopted extension to the 60+60 populations.

### Preserving overlapping patterns

[Yang and Doiron, 2025 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09296), subsequently published in PRX Life in 2026, analyze two overlapping assemblies whose synapses remain plastic after training (LOC-202). Nearly antisymmetric timing-dependent learning suppresses reinforcement from symmetric common-input correlations, allowing the groups to remain distinct in the tested regime. Inhibitory regulation, weight bounds, near-balanced learning and weak coupling are material assumptions. Causality and zero total learning-window area alone do not guarantee cancellation when positive and negative time courses differ (ANA-121). The reviewed full text is the preprint; the final article's abstract and publication status were verified. Preserved connection structure is a useful result, but many-pattern recall capacity and autonomous cycles are not demonstrated.

[Asabuki et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010214) discriminate sequences containing the same components in different orders through recurrently generated dendritic gating (LOC-203). This directly controls for unique component identity and occurrence frequency. The gating signal carries history; a separately trained additive recurrent pathway enables cued completion. Input weights also learn, and filtered potentials, normalization and internal compartments are essential state (AUD-064). The plotted neurons are sorted by response onset, so a sloping raster pattern is not an anatomical wave. Separate cues evoke separate completions rather than an indefinitely sustained cycle.

[Onasch et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.07.028) instead propose protecting overlapping assemblies through context-specific dendritic inhibition (LOC-205). The accessible published abstract and released source support this mechanism description; the full article and all final controls were not obtained. The inspected source supplies context identifiers and compartments, and normalizes combined feedforward/recurrent input weights (AUD-066). Thus externally selected context, internally inferred context and anatomical proximity are distinct explanations. None is a free consequence of the current minicolumn cell count.

[Zemliak et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44488-026-00029-5) provide another useful distinction: in both spatially inspired and nonspatial prewired networks, synchrony better identifies familiarity under varied input drive, while spike count better identifies the stimulated candidate pattern (LOC-204). The readout uses a supplied catalogue including novel candidates; it does not discover arbitrary new supports. More stored patterns also increase the union of recurrent connections, so the reported memory-load results do not hold degree fixed (AUD-065/ANA-123). Synchrony, mean activity, identity and familiarity are different quantities (ANA-122). The comparison supports functional recurrent organization without showing a need for physical coordinates or selecting a runtime synchrony mechanism.

### Local timescales and readable semantic persistence

[Zeraati et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-37613-7) find attention-dependent slow timescales in local V4 population activity and compare spatial recurrence with cellular alternatives (LOC-238/AUD-093). The experimental distance is receptive-field-center separation used as a proxy for cortical separation. Their model's local and global timescales differ; attention effects admit several changes in vertical and horizontal efficacy. This supports examining local temporal persistence, but does not identify a distance between semantic coordinates or a unique biological control mechanism.

The associated theory of [Shi et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.5.013005) explains how spatial interaction modes contribute different temporal components (LOC-242). Larger neighborhoods change degree while rescaling aggregate recurrent strength. The paper suggests a reservoir-computing benefit but does not demonstrate a learned task. Its stationary correlation theory, nonlinear operating-point analysis and discrete simulations have distinct assumptions. A correlation timescale is not a semantic memory duration or cycle period.

An independent finite calculation isolates the distinction at an exactly matched budget (ANA-151). Three 256-unit linear networks have identical degree, edge count, self-coupling and total incoming strength. Their global decay timescale is identical, while local integrated correlations differ substantially. Relabeling one graph preserves its entire spectrum but changes the response of an unchanged input/readout contrast. Thus even a demonstrated spectrum of slow modes leaves a necessary question: **do the fixed semantic inputs excite those modes, and do the required semantic observations retain their information?** No example here selects a graph or a semantic layout.

[Mosheiff et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010843) show that spatial E/I interactions can also generate shared chaotic variability under uniform drive (LOC-240). Two-dimensional sheets permit richer patterns in their model; the compared one- and two-dimensional simulations do not have equal total neuron counts. Shared noise and independent noise affect these dynamics differently. The work demonstrates a possible dynamical consequence, while leaving its computational usefulness open. Our activity envelope does not by itself require either suppressing or exploiting every form of microscopic chaos.

### Measured persistence and retained information

Persistence is not one interchangeable quantity. [Spitmaan et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2005993117) distinguish intrinsic fluctuations, task-epoch dynamics and reward/choice history in the same recordings (LOC-246). Regional hierarchies can resemble one another without significant within-area relationships between the individual-cell measures. [Wasmuht et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-05961-4) find that long baseline timescales support stable working-memory codes in selected populations, while faster cells can carry information in reproducible temporal patterns (LOC-247). Neither result identifies a physical relation between our assigned semantic coordinates. Useful retention must specify what information remains available and how its timing matters.

Measurement also requires care. [Zeraati et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-022-00214-3) show how finite-window mean estimation distorts autocorrelation and inferred timescales (LOC-248). ANA-152 verifies an exact example in which subtracting a short-record mean creates negative expected lag covariance although the true covariance is everywhere positive. That does not invalidate all timescale measurements; it means that negative measured covariance alone does not establish inhibition or alternating semantic states. No estimator is selected for the application.

[Shi et al., 2025, preprint v1](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.08.30.673281) report a broad slow-timescale tail across mouse brain regions (LOC-244). Their main multi-exponential analysis retains 11,468 of 27,960 neurons, and the fitted power-law tail is statistically indistinguishable from a lognormal alternative. A temporal exponent does not determine a degree or slot-capacity exponent. Even a network's exact decay spectrum does not determine its distribution of observed cellular timescales: ANA-153 preserves the spectrum and every nonzero edge count while changing the mixing of modes, producing either identical or heterogeneous cell autocorrelations. This is a counterexample to a universal inference, not a refutation of the paper's particular measurements or simulations.

[Stern et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.86552) supply a constructive nonspatial mechanism for temporal diversity: heterogeneous recurrent assemblies generate different switching times and preferential responses to input frequencies (LOC-245/AUD-094). The model's functional populations are not physical neighborhoods or automatically our minicolumns. Its rate units represent assemblies, its spiking realization uses additional E/I structure, and reservoir task training remains future work. Assembly dwell time, a cell's autocorrelation decay and the duration of a content-preserving semantic cycle therefore remain separate quantities.

### Structural benefits depend on the task

[Haeusler and Maass, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhj132) find that preserving neuron degrees and input/output roles nearly preserves average computational performance after laminar wiring is scrambled, while a memory-task deficit remains (LOC-239). Small-world controls alone perform worse. Their [2009 follow-up](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jphysparis.2009.05.006) tests a second anatomical template and finds task-dependent effects of feedback: a pathway can help rate computations while hurting particular pattern-memory tasks (LOC-243). These are trained-readout results with supplied recurrent weights and richer cell/synapse dynamics. They support distinguishing budgets, role alignment and temporal goals, not declaring degree sufficient for our learning problem.

[Wijesinghe et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2019.00504) report classification benefits from dividing some fixed-size spiking reservoirs into independent ensembles (LOC-241). Benefits depend on sparsity and reservoir size; the ordering can reverse. Their observation is a presentation-wide spike-count readout, and the equal-connectivity comparison primarily matches E-to-E edges. Independent reservoirs are therefore a useful counterexample to a universal need for a physical sheet, but are not adopted CC or minicolumn modules and do not establish sustained semantic recurrence.

[Balwani et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco.a.23) show that supplied hierarchical feedback and delays can make temporal violations decodable, even before task learning, in a six-population RNN (LOC-254/AUD-100). This is evidence about access to input history: the violation includes a repeated predecessor, so decoding it is not by itself evidence of a learned semantic expectation. The model learns input projections and readouts, and the inspected no-delay module changes recurrent factorization as well as timing. The printed random-projection guarantee also fails independent algebra checks (ANA-156), which limits quantitative transfer without negating the empirical comparisons. No within-population physical metric is needed for these effects.

[Kong et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.06.010) link CA3 recurrence to differing contextual coding strategies (LOC-256). The final abstract reports impaired distal context coding after local glutamatergic-transmission disruption; its intervention selectivity was not available for review. The separately inspected 2024 preprint uses RNN regularization and Hopfield comparisons to study recurrence. Its generalization comparison changes stored-pattern load with connectivity, while RNN regularization also changes weights. These are useful reasons to distinguish discrimination, robustness and memory capacity, rather than conclude that more or fewer neighbors is universally better. The older methods are not evidence about the new intervention's controls.

[Ghosh and Goodman, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44488-026-00013-z) compare all 128 combinations of optional recurrent pathways in a small sensorimotor architecture (LOC-258/AUD-102). Many partially recurrent networks match a fully recurrent model, including on tasks where a larger feedforward control fails. Advantages vary with cue availability and the pathway retained. This is positive evidence that access to the relevant history can matter more than retaining every recurrent route. It concerns learned blocks without a within-layer distance metric. Its memory measure is normalized lagged input sensitivity, its sample-efficiency measure counts training experience, and its resource comparison counts parameters; none is a direct measurement of autonomous semantic persistence or CPU energy. Independently trained architecture pairs also differ from acutely removing a connection in one trained network.

### Low-dimensional modes without a physical layout

[Pachitariu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10528-1) provide a complementary example of **retention without an imposed spatial map** (LOC-208/AUD-069). Near-critical symmetric random recurrence, ongoing noise and a trained decoder support delayed information retrieval. Their large mouse recordings show related variance spectra and relaxation modes. Sparse-positive, clustered and local variants can retain the broad spectrum, but the model also subtracts a dense inhibitory background. Its activity-covariance exponent is not a degree-distribution or capacity-class exponent.

This result separates useful persistence from self-sustained semantic cycling. Slower relaxation can amplify both the desired signal and ongoing noise; an illustrative scalar calculation has an intermediate optimum for delayed signal-to-noise ratio under fixed input and diffusion assumptions (ANA-127). The study's dominant spontaneous modes are largely nonrotational under its observation procedure, but truncated and single-lag dynamics estimates cannot universally exclude hidden rotations (ANA-126). Neither finding supplies a physical distance between our semantic roles or chooses the application's operating point.

[Cimeša, Ciric and Ostojic, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011315) construct low-rank recurrent structure in spiking networks and obtain fixed points, ring-like state organization and oscillatory modes without assigning neuron positions (LOC-153). The paper includes a sparse, sign-preserving control for externally driven unit-rank structure; that control should not be generalized to all of its rank-two oscillatory examples. Couplings are constructed rather than learned from fixed semantic input. Filtering changes which population geometry is observed, and a nonlinear firing-rate transformation need not preserve the exact low rank of the underlying activation coordinates (ANA-079).

A direct rate-model calculation makes the temporal distinction explicit (ANA-080): an isotropic rank-two interaction with a suitable antisymmetric component can produce rotation on a stable radius, whereas its symmetric counterpart can have a ring of stationary states. Mean population rate can remain constant in either case. Thus a ring-shaped point cloud and stable global activity do not identify recurrent cycling; directed dynamics and temporal order are indispensable. This conditional calculation does not establish sparse FP4 implementation, learned semantic trajectories or a reason to impose geometric distance on the neuron identities.

[Beiran et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_01381) distinguish the rank of recurrent structure from the number of populations defined by connection-loading statistics (LOC-174). Even one Gaussian population can support a stable oscillation; multiple populations permit a wider range of nonlinear dynamics. Their oscillator construction is a positive nonspatial example, but its finite-network accuracy depends on regularization and units per population. The published demonstration uses 2,000 units per population; those populations are not identified with our minicolumns. A theorem about approximating a continuous vector field does not establish preservation of long-term orbits or equivalence to a future FP4 update rule (ANA-097/098).

[Wan and Rosenbaum, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1103/79h2-36r3) sharpen the distinction between recurrent structure and response dimensionality (LOC-225/AUD-081). Strong low-rank recurrence can retain high-dimensional responses to high-dimensional drive, depending on the overlap between its input and output subspaces. Their spatial example supplies a periodic line and strong local negative feedback: coherent spatial inputs are suppressed more than disordered inputs. Thus a local kernel need not smooth activity. Its effect depends on feedback sign, input alignment and operating regime; these models do not learn an anatomical map for fixed semantic assignments.

Static response also leaves an important temporal question open. An independent two-variable example has stable decay and attenuates every constant input, yet amplifies a frequency band and has greater variance under white noise than the uncoupled system (ANA-142). This finite example does not contradict the cited asymptotic theory. It shows why a steady-state gain, a covariance dimension and a self-sustaining semantic loop require different evidence. Fixed allocated degree also does not automatically satisfy a theory whose structured coupling grows without bound as neuron count increases.

[Ebsch and Rosenbaum, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13408-020-00085-w) separate compatibility of the external profile with a spatial recurrent operator from positivity of firing rates and stability (LOC-224). These distinctions are relevant even though our model has no spatial profile. In one inspected example, the released input differs from the printed formula; for the released input, an exact signed solution exists but is negative near the boundaries. The applicable failure is positive-rate feasibility, rather than the paper's stated divergence argument (AUD-080/ANA-141). No claim about failure of all the paper's results follows. A mathematical cancellation condition alone is not sufficient for a realizable neuronal state.

### Fixed input roles and flexible internal computation

[Dubreuil et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01088-4) analyze task-dependent population structure through network resampling and gain modulation (LOC-175). The reviewed author-thesis chapter includes a successful control with fixed input directions and learned recurrent connections. This supports compatibility between assigned inputs and learned contextual behavior; it does not require that input roles be discovered through training. The fixed inputs are random distributed patterns, however, and overall amplitudes may be trained. They are not the application's signed semantic-coordinate populations.

The study's resampling preserves statistics of input, recurrent and readout loadings jointly, while replacing individual cells' loadings (AUD-045). Its success therefore establishes a distributional computational property, not preservation of each neuron's assigned role. Connection-space clusters and the nearest-neighbor angles used to detect them are analysis objects, not anatomical coordinates or a rule for choosing physical neighbors. The models use backpropagation and continuous dense recurrence; no biological Hebbian learning rule or static-slot mechanism follows from these results.

### Task-defined relations with fixed input roles

[Cazalets and Dambre, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-67137-1) provide a particularly relevant fixed-input comparison (LOC-213): each reservoir neuron receives one assigned input channel, while recurrence adapts through correlation-guided growth and random pruning. No anatomical coordinates are supplied. Dedicated channel groups outperform matched random input routing in 19 of 21 reported comparisons, although gains vary substantially. This supports the compatibility of stable input assignments with useful learned recurrent responses. The roughly 500-cell, continuously valued, driven reservoirs use a supervised readout; their results do not establish autonomous semantic loops or transfer to our signed FP4 populations.

The learning cost also needs separate treatment. The supplement estimates worst-case quadratic correlation work. Materializing one FP32 score for every unordered pair of our 614400 cells would require about 703.124 GiB; this is a conditional cost illustration, not a required implementation (ANA-133). Streaming and restricted candidate selection would need their own analysis. The paper's global-pair wording and target-wise pseudocode also differ: symmetric correlations alone do not guarantee reciprocal best-partner choices. These qualifications leave the positive fixed-input result intact without selecting its learning algorithm.

[Wagner et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2025.0260) offer a closer fixed-role sequence example (LOC-209/AUD-070). In the reviewed April 30 manuscript and released workflow, input/output matrices are frozen identities while recurrence and biases learn. A repeated moving-bump input on a ring produces learned recurrent structure supporting noise-driven replay and prefix-cued continuation. A task-aligned center-surround initialization improves the reported comparison. The authors distinguish representational progression from anatomical waves: **the circular relation belongs to the training task; it is not inferred from cell-body positions.** This supports learning functional transitions under stable input roles, while leaving overlapping dense semantic sequences and sparse FP4 transfer untested.

The evidence also clarifies evaluation. A refitted ring decoder can absorb a constant wrong phase, and concentrated angular errors need not be small; direct error measures remain necessary when the assigned readout matters (ANA-129). Mean neuronal gain is not a substitute for the full state-dependent Jacobian when assessing stability (ANA-130). The inspected timing-plasticity formula and source have specific qualifications recorded in AUD-070/ANA-128; the published successor's full methods were unavailable, so those qualifications are attached to the reviewed versions. No ring topology, symmetry ratio or timing rule is selected for this application.

[Larsen and Druckmann, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010227) provide a useful positive comparison: fixed pixel-input associations and task-defined neighboring connections let recurrent networks learn a connectedness computation that dense weight sharing alone does not reliably discover (LOC-190/AUD-054). The benefit needs sufficient processing steps; other architectures can be better under tighter latency. Pixel adjacency is part of that problem's definition. A corresponding graph relation has not been supplied by our embedding-coordinate labels. An exact Boolean reachability construction makes the distinction explicit: the computation needs the graph's edges, but no Euclidean coordinates once those edges are specified (ANA-111). This is an explanatory example, not a proposed SNN learning algorithm.

[Qian et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-70065-3) find that prescribed lateral mixing can produce cortical-like maps and improve some adversarial-robustness results in trained visual networks (LOC-191/AUD-055). Their filters learn around a supplied channel sheet, and the main mixing operation takes one step. The selected Gaussian model also trades recognition accuracy: 53% versus 69.57% for ResNet18. This supports a conditional benefit of grouping learned features, without identifying a valid smoothing relation between our fixed semantic coordinates or establishing autonomous recurrence.

### Exploring partners under a fixed capacity budget

[Kappel et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004485) demonstrate nonspatial cross-modal association and recovery using stochastic structural learning, predefined input routing and recurrent competition (LOC-172). Their [2018 reward-based model](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0301-17.2018) learns a cue-triggered two-step action and adapts after a task change (LOC-173). These are positive computational examples without a neuron-distance preference. Sampling connection parameters is nevertheless different from spontaneous cycling of represented semantic states, and the learning rules require priors, traces, fixed inhibitory structure or other assumptions that have not been chosen for this application.

The 2018 study explicitly tests replacing continuously evolving inactive-contact parameters with exponential waiting times, retaining similar eventual task performance. Such a memoryless process can mathematically aggregate independent timers rather than store one per absent pair (ANA-096). Identifying eligible partners and handling a full slot set remain separate problems. A statistical preference for sparse weights is not a hard per-neuron capacity constraint (ANA-095), and a per-contact exploration rate does not determine total search cost independently of the candidate universe. The public simulator's additional state is evidence about that implementation, not a proposed addition to our five-byte SoA slots (AUD-044).

### Sparse learning and candidate exposure

[Fruengel and Oberlaender, 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncir.2025.1528309) show task-specific benefits of fixed sparse random recurrence in large ReLU classifiers (LOC-250/AUD-096). Benefits depend on initialization, network size, activation and training duration. Inputs and readouts learn, and the release uses dense tensors with masks. Its illustrative sign-constraint code needs reconciliation with the stated rule. Thus reduced live connectivity can help learning without a physical metric, but sparse accuracy, sparse execution and preservation of our assigned input roles remain different claims.

[Knight et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1088/2634-4386/ae4535), reviewed from the January v2 manuscript and released source, provide an implementation of rewiring within preallocated sparse storage (LOC-251/AUD-097). Their classifier and spatial-map examples use different learning rules and have very different update costs. The spatial example already supplies positions and correlated sensory input; its successful map refinement does not discover a distance between semantic coordinates.

Two transfer checks are particularly relevant. One full potential-pair bitfield at 614,400 cells costs **43.945 GiB**, before live weights or other state; the reviewed classifier rule uses two such bitfields (ANA-154). Uniformly padding every row to the rare 18,432-slot maximum would likewise defeat the reason for capacity classes. These are conditional costs of external layouts, not additions to our memory budget. Further, global uniform proposals followed by a bounded-neighborhood acceptance rule can make per-neuron learning exposure decrease as the network grows, even when total attempts grow proportionally to neuron count (ANA-155). A faster simulator or equal elapsed simulation time does not alone establish an equally trained network. Neither calculation selects a candidate-search algorithm.

[Janzakova et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-43887-8) illustrate why material and computational locality must also be separated (LOC-252). Conducting-fiber growth has real distance and crossing constraints. Their small computational examples show benefits from activity-selected wiring, but combine that selection with geometry, supplied inputs or supervised label drive. Hardware growth, simulated task accuracy and biological anatomy provide different evidence; none supplies our missing physical metric.

### Shared regulation and preservation of useful differences

[Cecchini and Roxin, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10827-026-00932-x) demonstrate why stable activity fraction and useful response diversity must be distinguished (LOC-212). Their feedforward Hebbian model can repeatedly select the same high-input cells while maintaining a fixed active fraction. Target-specific inhibition prevents this freezing under the tested uniform-input assumptions. Here, “local” means one receiving neuron, without a physical neighborhood. For weighted input, subtracting a common degree-proportional term cancels the mean only when the connected sources' activity means match that term; covariance still affects response variation (ANA-132). This is evidence for examining the effect, not a decision to add a controller or stored statistics.

[Sweeney et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004389) model a diffusible homeostatic signal that permits heterogeneous firing rates and improves responses relative to their cell-local regulation controls (LOC-157). Connectivity and physical positions are assigned independently in the main example; diffusion acts on excitability, not partner selection. A reduced mixture of population and individual feedback captures part of the effect without a spatial field. This is a useful functional distinction, although it does not select either controller for the CC. In the printed reduction, any nonzero individual-feedback component eventually equalizes positive interior fixed-point rates under fixed inputs; heterogeneity over finite time and a heterogeneous asymptotic fixed point are different claims (ANA-082/083). Released-code normalization and finite calibration also qualify exact reproduction (AUD-039).

[Steinert et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.05.037) supply causal evidence that activity-dependent nitric oxide changes intrinsic excitability through potassium channels (LOC-159). Effects depend on cell type and timing; this does not establish one universal gain-reduction signal. [Slomowitz et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.04378) find recovery of population firing in cultured hippocampal networks while individual rates change substantially and short-term facilitation remains altered (LOC-158). These results support separate attention to population regulation, cell heterogeneity and retained computation. They measure firing rates and synaptic properties, not our FP4 phase fractions or semantic fidelity. Neither a new chemical field nor equal firing targets follow from them.

### Learning and maintaining assemblies during ongoing activity

[Ocker, Litwin-Kumar and Doiron, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004458) show how recurrent correlations and motifs shape timing-dependent weight changes without a physical layout (LOC-228/AUD-084). A nearly balanced timing window suppresses the independent-rate contribution, allowing correlations to guide learning. Common input can then change predictions made from an isolated neuron pair. Their broad-degree examples are constructed distributions, not anatomical estimates for our slot classes. Connections absent from the initial opportunity graph remain absent: learning their weights and discovering new partners are different problems.

[Ocker and Doiron, published online 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhy001) extend this approach to trained excitatory/inhibitory assemblies (LOC-229). In the pair-based example, learned structure reinforces itself after the training correlations are removed. The triplet-rule example retains correlated background input and maintains weights without the same spontaneous reinforcement. Stabilizing inhibition operates sufficiently fast relative to excitatory learning. These are positive demonstrations of maintained recurrent structure without a distance kernel, but the stimulus groups are assigned and disjoint; dense overlapping semantic patterns and autonomous ordered cycles are not demonstrated. The paper's spatial correlations refer to correlations across neurons, and its graph positions visualize connectivity rather than supply an anatomical metric.

Learning statistics also depend on time. Equal spike-count covariance can produce opposite expected timing-plasticity updates when causal order reverses (ANA-143). [Akil et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008958) show that rate and covariance terms, weight–rate correlations and finite-network fluctuations can matter differently across rules (LOC-226/AUD-082). A stable mean weight need not imply a stable variance, and regulating rates does not determine a unique learned matrix. [Andrei et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01446-w) provide complementary macaque perturbation evidence: repeated stimulation changes correlations while average evoked rates remain comparatively stable. Their model implicates inhibitory plasticity, with state and version limits recorded in LOC-227/AUD-083; this is not a direct measurement of every changing synapse or a definition of our DMN.

The assembly coding result is also narrower than a general semantic-memory claim. A selected summed-population readout improves because signal gain grows more than its noise. An independent linear example verifies that one fixed readout can improve while full-population information stays unchanged (ANA-144). Thus noise magnitude, response gain, information available to a particular readout, retained weights and a self-sustaining semantic trajectory must be assessed separately.

[Triplett, Avitan and Goodhill, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006421) show spontaneous assembly formation in a nonspatial network with covariance plasticity and repeated normalization of incoming and outgoing excitatory weights (LOC-220). Their spatial extension produces spatially organized groups by adding a distance-dependent weight cap; it also changes population size and parameters, so it is not an isolated test of computational advantage from geometry. Group counts persist while membership drifts, and their cued completion measurement freezes learning. The positive result is self-organization without a required physical layout. Stable semantic membership, ongoing learning and autonomous ordered cycles require additional evidence. The released basic code and published parameter tables differ in specific defaults, as recorded in AUD-078.

[Klos et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006187) combine several plasticity mechanisms to learn stimulus sequences and alter spontaneous replay (LOC-156). Their model already has a retinotopic sheet, distance-dependent growth and a moving spatial input; the result therefore cannot establish a distance metric for assigned semantic roles. Cue-order improvement is modest, and counting ordered local population peaks is not equivalent to demonstrating complete repeated cycles.

[Bergoin et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012973) offer a more direct nonspatial comparison: co-stimulation learns assemblies that spontaneously recur and maintain their weights while plasticity remains active (LOC-163). The model includes stable shared cells under overlapping stimulation, longer-duration examples and larger-network controls. Its two inhibitory plasticity classes regulate reinforcement and competition. The forgetting offset depends on the supplied number of memories, and the sparse control still retains 50% of possible connections. These successes warrant examination under our much sparser capacity and dense semantic inputs; they do not establish ordered vector cycles or require identifying positive/negative coordinate populations with excitatory/inhibitory cell classes. Exact-version code differences and the dependence of average weight drift on spike statistics are recorded in AUD-041/ANA-086. No learning rule or additional cell class is selected.

### Denoising must preserve legitimate multidimensional content

[Johnston et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1007544) show that mixed responses can improve reliable decoding, with the preferred degree of mixing depending on cell cost, noise location and the error objective (LOC-210/AUD-071). Their complete conjunction-code construction expands the population rapidly; improved separation is not free at a fixed cell budget (ANA-131). Stable semantic roles therefore leave room to investigate contextual responses, but generic mixed-selectivity benefits do not justify changing those roles or assigning a physical geometry.

[Vafidis and Rangel, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013672) demonstrate a different objective: a cue learns to evoke its associated outcome response through a compartment-local prediction error (LOC-211/AUD-072). Outcome mappings remain fixed, but cue mappings change; trial timing, teaching signals and a separately pretrained memory network contribute to the result. Delay conditioning succeeds even without recurrence. This separates useful predictive association from proof of autonomous recurrent computation, and synapse-local information from physical nearness. The tested Oja/BCM limitations do not establish that all coactivity learning is inadequate.

[Zajzon et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.77009) show that corresponding stimulus-group projections can improve signal reconstruction through successive recurrent populations (LOC-160). Their topography is a correspondence between groups, without neuron coordinates. Inhibitory-drive rescue, overlapping-map tests and dynamic-input examples provide useful controls. Competition can nevertheless suppress a weaker simultaneous signal, and the tested encodings differ from arbitrary dense coordinate vectors. For this application, a weak coordinate is not automatically noise. The valid-signal and corruption assumptions must establish what may be suppressed; an ideal identity mapping on all valid continuous variations cannot also contract those same variations as noise (ANA-084). Readout-delay selection and probability feasibility are recorded separately (AUD-040/ANA-085). No denoiser or inter-CC neuron projection is adopted.

### Degree, influence and dynamical stability

[Planert et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02134-7) identify a human temporal-cortex subtype with relatively frequent incoming connections, smaller initial somatic responses and lower excitability than another subtype (LOC-179). This is concrete support for separating input count from influence. Somatic response also depends on release, multiple contacts and membrane/dendritic properties, so the result does not establish a universal inverse-degree weight rule. The study shares data with Peng et al. (LOC-013), rather than supplying an independent cohort. Its released group-distance control averages only connected pairs: an independent calculation reproduces the published correlation and all sixteen connection-count entries (AUD-047). Conditioning on a successful connection does not describe the distribution of all tested opportunities, and equal group mean distances need not control a nonlinear distance effect (ANA-101). The subtype physiology remains useful evidence, with the distance comparison interpreted at its actual scope.

[Timme et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004858) associate outgoing functional degree with contributions to joint-input information, while incoming degree does not show the same association (LOC-161). These are inferred short-timescale interactions in partially observed cultures. The analysis requires individually detectable input edges and therefore does not include every possible joint dependency (ANA-068). It does not convert incoming allocated slots into a measure of a neuron's computational importance.

[Roxin, 2011](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2011.00008) shows that broadening actual incoming degree at fixed mean can suppress or generate oscillations in different circuits; response curves, inhibition and operating point determine the effect (LOC-162). [Vegué et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1162/netn_a_00442) predict heterogeneous stationary rates and plastic weights in specified regimes, accounting for biased sampling of connected endpoints, but explicitly leave stability and uniqueness of those stationary solutions unresolved (LOC-164). Their slow-rate approximation also excludes important timing-dependent plasticity cases. Independently, weaker weights can coexist with increasing receiving-neuron activity, and a low-noise rate estimate can require a long averaging time (ANA-087/088). These distinctions preserve training's responsibility for effective weights; they do not revise the static capacity allocation or establish an activity band.

[Dahmen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02395-w) give a quantitative example of recurrent motifs affecting covariance dimensionality (LOC-017/AUD-075). “Localized” refers to small connection motifs, not an inferred physical neighborhood. The supplement separates overall coupling from motif effects, uses a multi-population approximation and shows that these contributions can predict opposing layer trends. Its external-input controls use specified low-rank models; subtracting shared covariance does not generally identify where that activity originated.

The motif simulation measures the largest real part of connectivity eigenvalues. Continuous-time growth, eigenvalue modulus and stability of a chosen discrete update are different quantities (ANA-135). The dimensionality result concerns its stationary operating regime and covariance observation, rather than an activity-fraction bound or a universal semantic-loop criterion. Probed-edge masks, pooled cell-class probabilities and the random comparison's sampling assumptions also belong to the evidence boundary.

### Directed organization and temporal behavior

[Shao et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXLife.3.023008) show that directed chain motifs can alter feedback and population responses beyond what mean connectivity predicts (LOC-165). Their sparse, sign-constrained examples and fixed-count scaling are important controls: increasing neuron count does not inevitably increase the relevant feedback mode. Motif effects depend on which cell types participate, weight scaling and the dynamics being studied. Allocated slot capacity is not a substitute for those quantities.

Matching low-order motifs or stationary responses still does not determine temporal behavior. Graphs can share degrees and two-edge motif counts while containing different directed cycles; a reduction can preserve steady response but lose damped oscillatory responses (ANA-089–091). These are scoped counterexamples, not proposed networks. They support examining the organization of learned paths without requiring physical neuron coordinates, while keeping autonomous semantic cycling separate from stationary response prediction.

[Ghadiri et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0103-26.2026) demonstrate a timing-dependent distinction: forward connections favor shorter delays, while feedback connections favor delays compatible with the coupled circuit's resonance (LOC-182). Plasticity selects weights among fixed-delay connections; individual delays do not change. Reduced-density controls rescale weights and bounds, and large resonance mismatches weaken the feedback result. A periodic spike-pair calculation independently shows why an arrival-time learning term need not decrease monotonically with delay (ANA-102). This supports evaluating timing by its role in a recurrent path, rather than equating shorter transmission with universally better connectivity. The study does not establish semantic fidelity, long-term persistence across regime changes or an application delay-state budget.

[Zhang et al., 2026, preprint v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19949v1) report a conditional trade-off between reciprocal feedback, driven responsiveness and restoration toward a fixed point (LOC-193). Their main network converges to one bias-supported state, and a different spectral normalization removes the monotonic restoration result. The varied reciprocal support belongs to the excitatory component; the final signed operator also includes dense inhibition. These results do not establish sparse semantic cycling. At a fixed number `E` of directed edges, reciprocal fraction `ρ` fixes the number of distinct connected pairs as `E(1−ρ/2)`: more reciprocity necessarily reduces partner coverage, even when all incoming and outgoing degrees are preserved (ANA-115).

### Spatial sequence generation and what it establishes

[Spreizer et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1007432) provide a positive spatial construction: local connectivity with neighboring sources biased toward similar directions generates moving activity under independent external noise (LOC-183). Isotropic and independently randomized-direction controls remain localized; one uniform direction generates common-direction waves. The mechanism therefore involves coordinated directed pathways beyond distance decay. Its grid and directional field are prescribed, and its sequence detector uses those coordinates. No matched comparison establishes a 2D advantage for our fixed semantic roles.

The authors also distinguish evoked persistence from overlap with ongoing activity: strong background sequences can give a false impression that a stimulus response persisted. Moving activity alone is consequently insufficient evidence of the requested semantic recurrence. Their anatomical justification leaves the required spatial correlation of projection directions for further experiments. The released sampler additionally distinguishes radial contact draws, pairwise spatial density and distinct partner degree; repeated contacts are allowed (AUD-048/ANA-103). These details matter when comparing mechanisms at a fixed sparse capacity. Neither this construction nor its detector is adopted for the application.

### Maintaining memory with bounded synaptic state

[Lahiri and Ganguli, 2013](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2013/hash/7f24d240521d99071c93af3917215ef7-Abstract.html) analyze acquisition–retention tradeoffs in independent finite-state synapses (LOC-166). Their state count includes hidden internal states; it is not a neuron count or connection-slot count. The ideal-observer bounds concern a particular random-input and efficacy model, not the capacity of our correlated semantic network.

[Benna and Fusi, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4401) provide a positive nonspatial mechanism: interacting fast and slow variables retain memories despite low precision, and the paper reports noisy-cue retrieval in perceptron and fully connected recurrent controls (LOC-167). The main scaling results assume balanced, largely uncorrelated modifications. Several quantized internal variables supply more state than one expressed FP8 weight; their retention result cannot be assigned to that one byte without an equivalent mechanism. This does not establish that FP8 weights are insufficient for our task, or authorize additional state arrays.

[Urbizagastegui et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1088/2634-4386/ae01d2) report FP8 plasticity, and their public code contains directly rounded weight updates (LOC-168/AUD-042). The article remains abstract-only in this review; selected prepublication source was inspected without execution. Its numerical format, neural traces, event scheduling and dense recurrent experiment need separate assessment. Stochastic rounding can preserve small updates in expectation while adding variance; the inspected custom arithmetic is not exactly unbiased for every operand (ANA-092/093). Numerical resolution alone does not establish one-byte backing storage, a CC memory footprint or preserved nonlinear recurrent behavior.

### Local learning and transported error information

[Ellenberger et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66666-z) demonstrate online temporal learning using retrospective and prospective neuron dynamics (LOC-218). Local weight updates receive teaching errors through a feedback pathway; ideal feedback mirrors forward weights and complements temporal constants. Their positive results include classification and teacher-trained autoregressive forecasting. Hierarchical temporal processing and output-to-input feedback are distinct from the general lateral recurrent-learning problem left open in the paper.

The inspected preprint-era source implements matched feedback directly and carries continuous membrane, error-filter, rate and derivative states (AUD-076). It does not establish an FP4-only implementation or preserve every assigned input role. Its frequency-wise error phase agreement also does not guarantee the same instantaneous sign for a mixture of frequencies with unequal gains (ANA-136). These findings identify potentially useful computation and explicit information requirements. They do not select error populations, additional state fields, a training objective or a physical partner-selection rule for the CC.

### Locality within a target's dendrites

The relevant biological relation can be between **incoming synapses sharing a target**, rather than between neuron bodies. [Oh et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2014.12.016) found shrinkage and reduced surface AMPA-receptor signal at inactive spines near a stimulated cluster. Pharmacological dissociation supports active local signaling rather than a requirement that growing neighbors consume shared structural resources (LOC-169). This is conditional competition, not unconditional preference for closer partners.

[Govindarajan et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2010.12.008) found that strong stimulation could facilitate weaker potentiation, with effects depending on timing, dendritic distance and branch membership (LOC-170). Closely timed stimulated spines could also compete. Physiological-magnesium and acute-slice controls strengthen the evidence, but these experiments do not establish semantic memory content or autonomous recurrent activity. Different biochemical processes have different spatial and temporal ranges; they do not yield one universal locality kernel.

[Kirchner and Gjorgjieva, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-23557-3) model conditional cooperation and competition among incoming synapses (LOC-171). Changing the input ensemble produces orientation grouping in a ferret-like case and receptive-field-overlap grouping without orientation grouping in a mouse-like case. Thus functional grouping need not follow one feature label. Their model nevertheless supplies dendritic positions, visual receptive fields, traces and turnover that resamples inputs. Its reported organization is not a demonstrated benefit for our fixed semantic roles or three recurrent regimes. The inspected repository has unresolved helper/figure-path correspondence, so no exact reproduction is claimed (AUD-043).

Two source neurons can have adjacent contacts on one target and separated contacts on another. A single distance between those source identities cannot generally express both interactions (ANA-094). The transferable research question is what selective cooperation, competition or consolidation would accomplish for the application. Introducing compartments, shared resources, synaptic neighborhoods or another metric requires its own justified design; none follows automatically from these studies. Likewise, a particular model's white-noise control does not determine what our DMN noise can do: filtering and recurrent processing can produce correlated activity from temporally white drive (ANA-012).

[Shafiee et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-026-09719-3) further show how calcium spreading between nearby spines can yield either cooperation or competition, depending on timing, frequency, current weight and thresholds (LOC-198). A two-input model learns different responses to reversed input order while contact positions remain fixed. It is a dendritic learning interaction, not a global distance between neuron identities. Greater nearby influence need not mean potentiation: crossing different calcium thresholds can change the update's sign (ANA-119). Comparisons to experiments are qualitative, and the released example differs from printed parameter settings; a directory called `nodiff` actually retains diffusion while disabling plasticity (AUD-061). Neither that name nor the distance trend supplies a universal “nearer means stronger” rule.

[Humble, 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2025.1593837) supplies a positive example of persistent post-stimulation activity supported by local competition for a target's synaptic resources (LOC-266/AUD-104). Its 200-cell model learns weights within a supplied random mask. Neighboring inputs exchange weight resources; the released implementation orders them by their existing presynaptic IDs. That is an assumed arrangement of afferent synapses, not a measured distance between cells or our semantic populations. The stimulus is one period of independent Poisson drive, so persistence does not by itself establish discrimination among semantic memories or learned loops.

The resource mechanism also makes the meaning of a bound explicit. Transfers conserve the sum of incoming weights plus the target's resource reservoir; reservoir decay can only reduce it (ANA-165). This limits total weight, independently of the number of allocated slots, and does not specify a CC activity band. Such a reservoir is a proposed biological-resource state in that model, distinct from our static slot pools. No reservoir, afferent order or additional array is selected here.

### Retention, grouping and nonlinear operating range

[Tsimring et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-60825-y) followed developing mouse visual-cortex spines and found extensive turnover together with increasing local functional organization (LOC-187). Retained spines were more active, particularly for the soma's preferred direction. **Nearest-neighbor distance and correlation did not distinguish retained from lost spines** in the reported comparison; increasing local correlation therefore must not be retold as direct evidence that nearby coactive inputs preferentially survive. Their model combines Hebbian and heterosynaptic plasticity on supplied dendritic positions. Either mechanism can improve binocular alignment, while heterosynaptic interactions are needed for its local clustering. These are proposed mechanisms rather than experimentally isolated causes. Released source uses branch-specific kernels, traces and turnover at fixed sites (AUD-052). A stable average spine density is not evidence for our software pool design, and these dendritic relations do not define a distance between our semantic-coordinate cells.

[Tasciotti et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117793) compare reconstructed CA1 neurons with clustered large spines against within-domain shuffles preserving spine/input identities and their sizes (LOC-188). At moderate activation, local grouping increases somatic response and dendritic signaling; the somatic advantage diminishes at high activation. In supplied place-field input protocols, roughly 13% more activated inputs can restore a shuffled condition's firing. That is a conditional model result, not a measured CPU or energy saving. Input size and identity are shuffled together in the inspected source, so the control should not be criticized as merely changing their distribution (AUD-053). The model evaluates post-learning configurations, not a learned placement algorithm, and its dendritic conductances, compartment structure and external input patterns are essential assumptions.

[Basak and Narayanan, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1113/JP275310) instead find sharply tuned responses with inputs dispersed across an apical arbor (LOC-189). Their concentrated conditions place an entire input population on one or two branches. Low input can generate dendritic events that fail to drive the soma; high input can produce sustained depolarization that prevents useful spiking. Dispersed inputs allow cooperating dendritic events without those limits. They control unitary somatic input strength and perform conductance searches and channel-removal comparisons, but use one principal morphology and selected valid parameter sets. Their intervention potentiates chosen inputs externally; it does not demonstrate a learning algorithm. This contrasts with concentrating small groups across multiple branches in Tasciotti et al.; the two papers test different allocations and operating ranges.

The useful distinction is consequently **which inputs should interact, through what nonlinearity, and at what load**. A simple threshold-and-saturation calculation can favor concentration at low total drive and dispersion at high drive, with no change in total input (ANA-110). That counterexample is not a biological fit or an application proposal. It shows why neither “closer is always better” nor “dispersion is always better” follows from these results. Additional dendritic state would require a separate business justification and memory/compute assessment; it is not implied by our existing target-owned-slot proposal.

### Convergence on one receiving neuron

[Levi et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2022.111383) find improved signal reconstruction and spike timing in interneurons receiving convergent pyramidal inputs, with positive recruitment interventions and nonlinear coincidence-model controls (LOC-180). The external signal is shared, filtered noise; better recovery than an individual input is not information gain beyond the joint input population. The result supplies a useful target-centered relation without assigning global distances, but does not demonstrate independent-noise semantic cycling or uniquely identify the biological integration mechanism.

[Spivak et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adj4411) find coordinated transmission changes among inputs to the same PV interneuron (LOC-181). Peer timing predicts changes better than the individual pair's timing, with whole-assembly held-out controls; real stimulation adds timing-related predictive information absent from virtual-stimulation controls. These controls strengthen the finding. The measured quantity is effective spike transmission, influenced by synapses and receiving-cell state, rather than a stored weight alone. This concerns existing inputs and does not establish a new-partner preference. Target-local integration, coordinated learning and spatial proximity remain distinct questions; no incoming-slot ownership or auxiliary state is approved by this evidence.

### Testing a claimed computational advantage of geometry

[Shakiba et al., 2026, preprint v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14975v1) report improved decision-task learning from MICrONS-derived functional initialization and spatial regularization (LOC-147). This is a relevant positive computational comparison. Its released code, however, normalizes real coordinates to a unit cube while retaining raw integer grid coordinates in the control. For the main 312-node grid, mean pair distance is **6.5746**, compared with **0.8713** after normalizing that grid's axes. Equal penalty coefficients therefore do not isolate geometry from penalty scale. The plain-L1 implementation also omits its stored coefficient. These findings require reconciliation before attributing the reported advantage uniquely to cortical placement; they do not establish how much of the benefit would survive corrected controls (AUD-035/ANA-074).

The same study retains good performance after jointly permuting rows and columns of initial weights. That operation preserves the weighted graph and its spectrum while changing its correspondence with fixed positions. It does not test a weight histogram alone; the separately reported edge-value resampling comparison addresses a different question (ANA-075). Learned input and readout weights, dense ReLU recurrence and supervised training also differ materially from our fixed semantic roles and static sparse capacity.

[Beiran and Litwin-Kumar, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-025-02080-4) show why structural and functional acceptance must stay separate (LOC-148). Networks with identical wiring can match a task output while differing in individual-neuron dynamics; adding suitable activity observations can resolve much of that ambiguity under known-input and model assumptions. A biologically plausible graph can therefore be informative without determining the required recurrent behavior. Our comparison must identify which semantic trajectories and state properties need to be preserved, rather than treating anatomical similarity as sufficient.

[Beyh et al., 2026, preprint v2](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.07.742341) supply stronger positive geometry controls: anatomically constrained parcel networks learn faster than matched input/output masks alone and produce spatial response subspaces that correspond better to empirical fMRI than retrained spatial-permutation nulls (LOC-192/AUD-056). The unconstrained network nevertheless learns the task better and faster. The anatomical regularizer correlates signed weights with proximity and also changes directional symmetry incentives; it is not solely a per-edge length penalty (ANA-112). Explained spatial variance is invariant to reordering the empirical time samples, so this endpoint does not validate temporal sequence prediction (ANA-113–114). The result concerns parcel geometry relative to anatomical input/output sites, which our semantic neurons do not possess.

Reservoir comparisons also show why scores and operating conditions must remain explicit. [Damicelli et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010639) find that anatomical masks with random signed weights can match random reservoirs, while other weight assignments perform worse (LOC-194/AUD-057). [Suárez et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-021-00376-1) report an anatomical advantage near a selected dynamical regime and under an anatomical wiring-cost adjustment (LOC-195/AUD-058; primary preview/captions and released source inspected, full final article unavailable). Their input/readout placement, nulls and memory scores differ. Summed absolute correlations and summed squared correlations can rank networks oppositely; only the latter carries the usual linear memory-dimension interpretation (ANA-117). Anatomical weight-times-distance cost is also distinct from our slot memory and CPU execution cost.

[Mackey et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44387-025-00013-x) offer a particularly relevant positive comparison (LOC-257/AUD-101). Distance-dependent magnitude penalties and progressive pruning outperform penalties with shuffled coefficients in several conditions. The preferred manifold depends on the task, and language results do not clearly select one topology. Fixed neuron positions still permit learned input mappings and hidden meanings; the study therefore does not hold our semantic assignments fixed. Its coefficient shuffle preserves the histogram while changing reciprocal symmetry, diagonal penalties and row exposure (ANA-158). The gains establish useful pairwise structure under the tested training conditions, without isolating physical distance as the unique cause. The annular penalty also serves an optimization purpose and is not anatomical evidence for our very-near exception.

Two further distinctions matter for transfer. The method trains dense weights before pruning and uses dense masked arrays, so it supplies no measured CPU or bounded-candidate-search advantage. Also, a magnitude penalty does not determine the signed recurrent operator: matrices with identical absolute weights can have different stability and memory properties (ANA-157). These limits preserve the empirical result while preventing a shape preference from becoming an unsupported application rule.

[Biswas and Fitzgerald, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.4.023255) provide a useful inverse perspective (LOC-260): specified neuronal responses constrain an ensemble of possible recurrent weights, and additional weight bounds can make some synapses necessary. Their geometry is in response/weight space, not a layout of cells. The framework explicitly leaves stable fixed points and further biological constraints as refinements. A small independent example preserves the supplied input, fixed response, connection support, source signs and a common weight bound while changing from stable to unstable local dynamics (ANA-160). Matching semantic responses or a graph statistic therefore cannot replace checking the required temporal behavior.

### Unresolved comparison requirements

The research has not identified a single mechanism satisfying all three regime goals under the fixed semantic and static-capacity constraints. A useful comparison must distinguish:

- Geometry's effects from changes in live degree, weight scale, common input, delays, inhibition and learning budget.
- Prescribed connectivity from connectivity actually learned with a sparse, bounded partner-search process.
- Stimulus-driven activity, silent memory, one-pass replay and autonomous repeated semantic loops.
- A bounded active fraction from the identity, ordering and semantic quality of the active patterns.
- Regime-level attraction and convergence versus the finite token-specific response; longer persistence is not automatically better when timely token fading under the loose half-life-like objective is the intended outcome.
- Preservation of coordinate roles from preservation of trained response profiles or only final task accuracy.
- Intended autonomous recurrence from loss of external-token steerability; long residence or a repetitive path alone does not establish stale behavior, and stronger persistence is insufficient if required input responses are lost.

These are evidence requirements for discussion. H-52's approximate duration is retained as the behavioral target; additional arrays, topology, learning algorithms and exact experimental acceptance thresholds remain unselected.
